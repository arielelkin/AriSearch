## New primary theorem: fifteen geometric Penrose maps

Thomas Fernique and Victor Lutfalla, *Geometrical Penrose tilings are
characterized by their 1-atlas*, TCS 1022 (2024), article 114883,
[publisher full text](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304397524005000),
[arXiv:2211.08239v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08239).
Theorem 1 and Proposition 1 assert that an edge-to-edge tiling by unit
thin/thick rhombi is geometrical Penrose precisely when every radius-1
vertex map belongs to the fifteen maps shown up to isometry in Figure 8.
Radius means graph edge-distance: a 1-map contains every tile having a
vertex at distance at most one from the chosen vertex. Lemma 3 restores
valid Penrose decorations from these neighborhoods.

The seven undecorated radius-0 stars do not suffice; Figure 7 gives a
whole-plane non-Penrose tiling satisfying that smaller atlas. Thus a route
that enforces only Penrose corner multisets or radius-0 stars cannot import
this theorem. Shape-only enforcement of the fifteen maps, and exclusion
of non-edge-to-edge or monohedral escape tilings, remain separate duties.
The theorem is a finite forcing target, not a geometric two-polygon
construction. It does not validate Ammann's convex recomposition by itself.
