# Literature boundary: two unmarked convex aperiodic polygons

**Search date and cutoff:** 2026-10-02.

## Result within this boundary

No primary source located constructs an aperiodic protoset of two unmarked
convex polygons under unrestricted contacts and isometries, and no located
source proves such a pair impossible.  This is a literature status report,
not a proof that no result exists.

## Closest primary sources

1. Teruhisa Sugimoto, *Aperiodic sets of three types of convex polygons*,
   arXiv:2404.00534v5 (updated 2025-01-15),
   <https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.00534>.  Its introduction poses whether two or
   one convex prototiles without matching conditions are possible.  Its
   conclusion reports no known two-type example and apparently no
   nonexistence proof.  Crucially, its own new constructions treat front and
   back as different types and forbid reflections.  They therefore do not
   meet this problem's definitions.  Section 2 states more strongly that the
   divided Tile(1,1) sets generate periodic tilings when reflected pieces are
   allowed, so they are explicit non-candidates under the present
   convention.  The paper also says those new
   three-type constructions are not confirmed actually to be aperiodic
   sets.

2. Teruhisa Sugimoto, *Convex Polygons for Aperiodic Tiling*,
   arXiv:1602.06372v2; RCMMS 8 (2017), 69--79,
   <https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.06372>.  It proves that a convex polygon which
   admits an **edge-to-edge monohedral tiling** admits a periodic tiling.  It
   does not cover non-edge-to-edge monohedral tilings or mixed two-shape
   tilings.  It describes the classical Ammann recomposition into two convex
   pentagons and one convex hexagon and poses the at-most-two problem.

3. Teruhisa Sugimoto, *Convex pentagonal monotiles in the 15 Type families*,
   arXiv:2501.07090v2 (updated 2026-01-29),
   <https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.07090>.  Its conclusion calls the assertion
   that every unmarked convex pentagon which tiles admits a periodic tiling a
   conjecture, and says Rao's claimed exhaustive classification has not been
   definitively established.  Its theorem again covers the edge-to-edge
   monohedral case only.

4. Branko Gruenbaum and G. C. Shephard, *Tilings and Patterns* (1987),
   pp.580–582, is cited by Sugimoto for Ammann's convex construction.
   Sugimoto also cites Hallard T. Croft, Kenneth J. Falconer, and Richard K.
   Guy, *Unsolved Problems in Geometry* (Springer, 1991), pp.79–80,
   95–96 (C14), 101–103 (C18). The earlier entry here identifying an
   “I. Hallard” Cambridge thesis was a bibliographic error, corrected on
   2026-10-09 against [Sugimoto's primary reference list](https://arxiv.org/html/1602.06372v2#bib.bib5).
   The reflection convention still has to be audited before the convex
   benchmark is used as a source set for the present problem.

5. Robert Ammann, Branko Gruenbaum, and G. C. Shephard, *Aperiodic Tiles*,
   Discrete & Computational Geometry 8 (1992), 1--25,
   DOI `10.1007/BF02293033`, is a rigorous primary treatment of the Ammann
   A2--A5 marked/concave families.  It is **not** a proof for the later
   two-pentagon-plus-hexagon convex recomposition: the paper points to book
   pages 550--558 for its families, whereas Sugimoto cites pages 580--582 for
   the convex construction.  It must not be imported as the missing
   all-tilings proof for the convex benchmark.

6. Ze Zhu, Erxiao Wang, and Min Yan, *Hexagonal Tiling of the Plane*,
   arXiv:2508.01404v2 (2026), <https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.01404>.  Its Main
   Theorem proves that a congruent hexagon tiling in which at least three
   tiles meet at every vertex uses a Reinhardt-family hexagon; convexity makes
   that vertex hypothesis automatic.  The proof explicitly treats half
   vertices/non-edge-to-edge contacts and flips.  The authors also identify
   gaps in the historical Reinhardt and Bollobas arguments, so those older
   citations should not alone be called complete proofs.  This was an arXiv
   preprint, not a located peer-reviewed publication, at the search cutoff.

7. Teruhisa Sugimoto, *Converting tilings with multiple types of rhombuses to
   pentagonal tilings*, arXiv:2203.04457v3,
   <https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.04457>.  Its Penrose conversion uses exactly
   two convex pentagon shapes when $54^\circ<\theta<90^\circ$, but each is
   a Type-2 periodic monohedral tile.  It therefore fails the target's
   monohedral-escape gate; see the [dedicated audit](penrose-to-two-pentagons-audit.md).

8. Teruhisa Sugimoto, *Tile sets consisting of two types of concave polygons
   derived from periodic tilings corresponding to non-periodic tilings with
   hat and turtle tiles*, arXiv:2609.09603v1,
   <https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09603>.  This September 2026 preprint is the
   latest two-type hit in the expanded convex-pentagon query, but its four
   proposed pieces are explicitly concave and the abstract describes the
   two-piece aperiodicity only as a possibility.  It does not meet the convex
   target or supply a contrary resolution.

## Audit of the classical convex benchmark

The accessible Sugimoto 2017 presentation supplies the relations
\(GL=DJ\), \(FL=CJ=EK\), and \(HK=BJ\), with
\(DJ,CJ,BJ,KL\) pairwise distinct, but it supplies neither exact coordinates
nor a proof that every unrestricted tiling reconstructs a Penrose tiling.
Sugimoto 2025 explicitly observes that no reflected pieces occur in the
displayed Ammann tiling and then adopts a no-reflection convention for its
own constructions.  That observation does not prove that reflected copies
are harmless.  The displayed tiling is edge-to-edge, but there is likewise
no accessible proof there excluding other tilings with T-junctions.

Accordingly, the classical three-convex-polygon set is retained only as a
promising literature lead, not as a certified strongly aperiodic source set
under the target definitions.

## Consequences

* Monohedral classification does not decide the mixed-pair question.
* Edge-to-edge results do not cover unrestricted partial-edge contacts.
* Chiral/no-reflection constructions do not cover the permitted reflections.
* Because a tiling may use only one member, each candidate polygon must
  individually avoid every periodic monohedral tiling.
* Any novelty claim must be relative to this recorded arXiv/OpenAlex boundary.
