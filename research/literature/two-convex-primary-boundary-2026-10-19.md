# Bounded primary-source boundary for two unmarked convex polygons

Checked 2026-10-19. This is a novelty boundary, not a completeness claim.

Search queries: `aperiodic pair convex polygons unmarked two tiles open problem 2025 2026`; `aperiodic set two convex polygons 2024 2025 paper`; `two convex aperiodic tiles 2026`; `site:arxiv.org aperiodic two convex polygon tiling 2025 2026`; `site:arxiv.org two convex polygon aperiodic pair tiling plane 2025 2026`; `two convex prototiles aperiodic tiling reflection allowed 2026 research`; `site:arxiv.org aperiodic set two convex polygons`; `"heptagon" "144" "126" "pentagon" tiling`; `"regular pentagon" heptagon 126 144 tiling`.

* Teruhisa Sugimoto, [*Aperiodic sets of three types of convex polygons*, arXiv:2404.00534v5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.00534), describes four dissections of Tile(1,1) into five convex pieces of three classes. Its Section 1 explicitly treats front and back as different types and assumes reflected pieces cannot be used; Section 2 says reflected Tile(1,1) admits a periodic tiling and that the resulting three-piece systems admit periodic tilings with reflections. It therefore does not supply a pair under this repository's unrestricted isometry convention. The paper states the two-convex question as open within its convention; that statement is not a proof of present-day status.
* Teruhisa Sugimoto, [*Tile sets consisting of two types of concave polygons derived from periodic tilings corresponding to non-periodic tilings with hat and turtle tiles*, arXiv:2609.09603v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09603), studies concave two-type systems and discusses possible aperiodicity. Its abstract does not assert a two-convex construction or unrestricted impossibility theorem.
* David Smith, Joseph Samuel Myers, Craig S. Kaplan, and Chaim Goodman-Strauss, [*An aperiodic monotile*, arXiv:2303.10798](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10798), supplies the nonconvex hat and its hierarchy. Convex repartitions require a new shape-only forcing proof. Their [*A chiral aperiodic monotile*, arXiv:2305.17743](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17743), distinguishes the equilateral polygon Tile(1,1), which has a periodic tiling with reflections, from its curved-edge Spectre modification. A finite partition of the curved parent into polygons cannot preserve its curved boundary; a point-site dualization would be a different construction with independent class-count and forcing obligations.
* Ze Zhu, Erxiao Wang, and Min Yan, [*Hexagonal Tiling of the Plane*, arXiv:2508.01404v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.01404), Main Theorem, proves that a congruent-hexagon plane tiling with at least three tiles at each vertex must use a Reinhardt H1/H2/H3 hexagon; the paper explicitly covers non-edge-to-edge contacts, and a convex hexagon meets its vertex hypothesis. We use this primary classification only for monohedral omission checks in the new 39-case hexagon grid. It does not classify two-species tilings.
* José Cáceres and Alberto Márquez, [*An aperiodic tiles machine*, Computational Geometry 23 (2002), 171–182](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0925-7721(01)00060-8), describes an algorithm producing aperiodic tiling examples via Voronoi diagrams. Only its abstract was inspected in this refresh; it establishes prior art for the general Voronoi approach, not a two-unmarked-convex-class result or a match to any exact site rule here.
* Teruhisa Sugimoto, [*Convex Polygons for Aperiodic Tiling*, arXiv:1602.06372](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.06372), states in its abstract that a convex polygon generating an edge-to-edge monohedral tiling also generates a periodic tiling. This is a monohedral edge-to-edge statement and does not decide an unrestricted two-species pair. Only the abstract was inspected in this refresh.

No claim here excludes other literature or establishes novelty of any prospective result. Any final solution requires a fresh comprehensive primary-source audit against its exact construction or theorem.
