# Seven children: exact interior-count gate for a future topology attack

Date:2026-10-13. Analytic working result; independent review pending.
Let a convex polygon be partitioned into exactly seven genuine convex children,
all having at least five native sides. Call a child interior when it has no
positive-length parent-boundary contact (isolated boundary points allowed).
The planar adjacency graph with exterior vertex uses only positive contacts.
Every interior child has at least five distinct child neighbors, by convex
intersection and its five genuine side directions.

Exactly two interior children are impossible. Of the five outer children,
each interior meets at least four, so the two interior neighbor sets share
at least three outer children. Those two interiors and the exterior vertex
versus three common outer neighbors give a K3,3 subgraph.

Exactly three interior children are impossible. Each meets at least three
of the four outer children, after allowing its other two neighbors to be
interior. Drop all interior-interior and outer-outer edges and retain the
bipartite graph between the three interiors plus exterior and the four outer
children. It has eight vertices and at least9+4=13edges. A simple bipartite
planar graph on eight vertices has at most12edges, contradiction.

This proves only exclusion of interior-counts two and three; it does NOT
exclude a seven-child dissection, and does not classify one or four-or-more
interiors. For a pentagon parent with six pentagon children and one hexagon,
the exact boundary-defect budget is B+2J+2D+H=5. A proposed one-interior
exclusion still needs analysis of a central pentagon with one outer child
not touching it, or a subdivided central native side with six neighbors.
The six-child root-forest argument cannot simply be copied because not all
outer children need touch each central native side. Four-or-more interiors
remain a separate finite planar/topological obligation.

A genuinely new classification could improve the current seven-child minimum
for a pentagon plus a higher-sided partner. No such improvement is asserted.
No computation or novelty claim is made.
