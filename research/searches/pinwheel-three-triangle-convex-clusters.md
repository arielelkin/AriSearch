# Convex three-triangle pinwheel recomposition gate

Date: 2026-10-13. Status: exact finite computation, independently hostile-reviewed VALID; [review](../reviews/pinwheel-three-triangle-convex-clusters-review.md). Not an aperiodic pair or a complete compiler classification.

## Question and finite domain

Can a convex polygon supported by three congruent 1-2-sqrt(5) right triangles, connected through full native sides, serve as a member of the target pair without a monohedral periodic escape? The domain includes arbitrary rotations and reflections of the triangles, with a connected full-side adjacency graph. Every three-piece patch is generated by adjoining a second triangle along a side of a fixed base triangle and then a third along a side of either existing triangle. Interior overlap is rejected exactly. The generator overincludes patches with additional partial contacts; therefore an exclusion for its entire output also excludes the edge-to-edge subdomain. No arbitrary partial-side attachment is enumerated. The independent checker additionally confirmed that all36 generated three-piece patches are edge-to-edge.

The base triangle is T=((0,0),(2,0),(0,1)). Its three side lengths are distinct, so matching a complete side between two congruent copies leaves exactly two placements on the opposite side: reflection across the side line and half turn about its midpoint. There are no other isometric placements with that full-side support. A connected three-vertex adjacency graph has an ordering obtained from a spanning tree, so this generates every patch in the domain up to an isometry taking the first triangle to T.

All coordinates remain rational. Disjoint interiors are tested by the separating-axis theorem on native edge normals, with equality permitting boundary contacts. A convex hull of area3 has exactly the area of three interior-disjoint unit-area triangles, so equality implies the closed hull is their union: any uncovered relative open subset would have positive area, and a closed finite union cannot miss a boundary point without also missing adjacent interior points. Congruence is reduced by complete rational squared-distance matrices over all cyclic shifts and reversals of the convex hull vertex cycle.

## Retained result

The [producer](pinwheel-three-triangle-convex-clusters.py) emits the [exact certificate](../certificates/pinwheel-three-triangle-convex-clusters.json). Raw distinct anchored patches at one, two, and three pieces are1,6,36. Exactly five convex congruence classes occur: four genuine quadrilaterals and the pentagon

    P=((0,0),(6/5,-8/5),(2,-1),(2,0),(0,1)).

All five classes have periodic monohedral escape. For the quadrilaterals use the general quadrilateral carrier construction in [the side-count lemma](../lemmas/side-count-and-monohedral-obstructions.md). For P, its successive edge vectors are

    (6/5,-8/5),(4/5,3/5),(0,1),(-2,1),(0,-1).

The entering edge at corner3 is (0,1), and the leaving edge after corner4 is (0,-1). The two consecutive strictly convex exterior turns consequently sum to pi; their corresponding interior angles sum to pi. The half-turn union across the side between those two corners is a convex centrally symmetric hexagon, which tiles by a lattice. Keeping its two P copies gives a monohedral period, as proved in [the supplementary escape](../lemmas/pentagrid-pentagon-angular-reduction.md). This uses allowed isometries and omits any proposed partner, as the problem permits.
