# Audit: converting the two Penrose rhombi to two convex pentagons

**Status:** ruled out immediately by monohedral escape.

Teruhisa Sugimoto, *Converting tilings with multiple types of rhombuses to
pentagonal tilings*, arXiv:2203.04457v3, gives a particularly close-looking
construction.  In Section 5.1 the thin and thick Penrose rhombi (acute angles
$36^\circ$ and $72^\circ$) are converted using two pentagon parameters
labelled $n=10$ and $n=5$.  For $54^\circ<\theta<90^\circ$, both
resulting shapes are convex pentagons.  Thus the displayed converted Penrose
pattern really is a tiling by two convex pentagon shapes.

It is not an aperiodic protoset.  The paper's construction is a conversion of
a chosen rhombic pattern, not a proof that every tiling by the pentagons
reconstructs matching-rule Penrose rhombi.  More decisively, Section 2 states
that each convex pentagon produced by the conversion belongs to the Type-2
monohedral family, with angle relation $B+D+E=360^\circ$ and the associated
side equalities.  Such a pentagon has a periodic monohedral tiling.  Since the
target definition permits a tiling to omit the other member, either pentagon
alone supplies a periodic tiling admitted by the pair.

This example cleanly separates two obligations: converting one nonperiodic
source tiling into two convex shapes proves neither shape-only forcing nor
monohedral escape.  It should not be revived as a candidate without changing
the shapes enough to destroy their Type-2 periodic assemblies, at which point
existence and reconstruction would both need new proofs.
