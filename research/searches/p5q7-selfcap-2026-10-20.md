## Four-face full-edge periodic torus attack

For a 2P+2Q **edge-to-edge translational torus**, Q's length-`d` side must meet a Q length-`d` side: it cannot meet either P length-`d` side, since one endpoint then has `150+135=285` degrees and the remaining 75 degrees is absent from the angle palette. The two Q are therefore paired across side 0, and their four 150-degree corners require the two P 60-degree corners as the two caps. The eight base patches above exhaust that prescribed 2P+2Q full-edge arrangement, including reflected Q and both P assignments.

Necessary boundary edge-vector balance fails for six of the eight bases. It holds only for a half-turn Q seam and off-diagonal P caps `(0,1)` or `(1,0)`. In `(0,1)`, the required Q/Q placement across the unique side 2 overlaps the far P cap, so it cannot be a torus. In `(1,0)`, let `T_u` and `T_w` be the translations pairing the two Q copies' unique sides 2 and 4. In any flat translational torus these must lie in its period lattice. Exact area computation gives `1 < det(T_u,T_w)/(2 area(P)+2 area(Q)) < 2`. The quotient is required to be an integer lattice index, contradiction. The script emits the exact field element and checks the strict bounds. Thus **no 2P+2Q edge-to-edge translational torus** exists for these exact shapes.

This excludes one small periodic carrier only. A larger periodic tiling, a partial-contact periodic tiling, a nonperiodic infinite tiling, or no plane tiling at all remain possible. No global forcing or extension mechanism is known, so this fixed metric is stopped. Reopen only with a concrete plane extension or intrinsic contact invariant, not a larger bounded patch search.
