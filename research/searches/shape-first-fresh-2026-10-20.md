## Geometry and deduplication

For a positive cyclic side word `w=(w0,...,w5)`, set `v0=(0,0)` and
`v(k+1)=vk+wk uk`, where `uk=(cos(k pi/3),sin(k pi/3))`. In the exact
triangular basis `e0=(1,0), e1=(1/2,sqrt(3)/2)`, the six `uk` have integer
coordinates `(1,0),(0,1),(-1,1),(-1,0),(0,-1),(1,-1)`. Closure is exactly
`w0-w3=w2-w5=w4-w1`. Positive closed words give strictly convex
120-degree hexagons.

The three trials are:

| 2 | `(1,4,3,2,3,4)` | `(2,4,4,3,3,5)` | rank-two periodic tiling |
| 3 | `(1,2,3,2,1,4)` | `(1,3,3,2,2,4)` | no plane tiling |

All displayed words close. Trial 1's A word has opposite difference `-2`; the other displayed words have difference `-1`. They are positive,
noncentral, strictly convex, and have
length multiplicities `(2,2,1,1)`.
The trial-2 pair is inequivalent, under independent dihedral word changes
and species swap, to the prior private-dimer seed

## Trial 1: exact local contradiction

A's length-1 side occurs once and is absent from B, so it must pair with
another A length-1 side. A is mirror-symmetric about the perpendicular
bisector of that side; the nominal reflection and half-turn mates give the
same geometric AA pair. At either seam endpoint, two A corners consume
240 degrees and expose two length-4 sides. The third corner must be B's
unique `(4,4)` corner because A has no `(4,4)` corner.

Follow the two B length-4 sides to their far endpoints. The two A sides
immediately after the matching length-4 sides both have length2, whereas
the B sides immediately after its length-4 sides have lengths2 and3.
Whichever of the two reflected B caps is chosen, one far endpoint therefore
requires a third `(2,2)` corner. Neither tile has that corner. Thus no
tiling can contain A. A B-only tiling is also impossible: B's unique
length-5 side forces a BB seam; its half-turn endpoint needs `(2,3)`,
absent from B, while its reflected seam needs `(2,2)`, also absent.
Therefore trial 1 admits no plane tiling. This directly kills the fixed
pair despite a finite nonoverlapping initial patch.

## Trial 2: local patch and periodic carrier

A's private length1 and B's private length5 again prevent either species
from tiling alone: an AA seam needs B's `(4,4)` corner; a BB half-turn
seam needs A's `(2,3)` corner, while the reflected BB seam needs the absent
`(2,2)` corner. A's private-side mate is geometrically unique by its mirror
symmetry. Unlike trial 1, A's next sides after its two length-4 arms are
length3; B's next sides are2 and3. The required `(2,3)` and `(3,3)`
corners are present. An AA pair, two B seam caps, and their two BB mates
form a six-tile patch with pairwise disjoint interiors. The replay checks
that fact by integer separating axes. It proves only local compatibility.

The following four-state symbolic honeycomb carrier is decisive. Write
`state=(tile,r,eps)`, where the side of global heading `k` has length
`w_tile[(r+eps*k) mod 6]`; `eps=+1` or `-1` includes both reflection
parities. Use quotient cells `(x,y)` modulo the HNF lattice generated by
`(2,0)` and `(1,2)`:

| cell | state |
| --- | --- |
| `(0,0)` | `(B,4,+1)` |
| `(1,0)` | `(A,5,-1)` |
| `(0,1)` | `(B,1,+1)` |
| `(1,1)` | `(A,2,-1)` |

For each of the four cells and each heading `k=0,1,2`, the side length
equals the neighbor's side of heading `k+3`. The replay checks all twelve

## Trial 3: all-shared corner-demand obstruction

This trial has no private side length: both polygons use exactly
`{1,2,3,4}`. It was selected from the all-shared four-length lane,
outside the private-dimer mechanism. The A word does not occur verbatim
in the prior research inventory. Its mixed `(1,2,4)` vertex star is
locally legal. A finite HNF honeycomb screen found no quotient of index
at most6, but that negative screen has no role in the proof below.

Let `N_A(R),N_B(R)` count tiles whose chosen reference points lie in a
radius-`R` disk. Because both shapes have bounded diameter and positive
area, counts of tiles in a fixed-width boundary annulus are `O(R)` and
`N_A(R)+N_B(R)=Omega(R^2)` in any plane tiling.

The only possible third side label at a trivalent vertex containing a
`(1,4)` corner is `2`: neither shape has `(1,1)`, `(3,4)`, or `(4,4)`,
while `(1,2)` and `(2,4)` occur. Every `(1,4)` corner therefore belongs
to a `(1,2,4)` star and requires one `(1,2)` corner and one `(2,4)`
corner. These latter corner types may also occur in other stars, so the
valid finite-patch comparisons are inequalities:

    # (1,4) corners <= # (1,2) corners + O(R),
    # (1,4) corners <= # (2,4) corners + O(R).

A has two `(1,4)`, two `(1,2)`, and zero `(2,4)` corners. B has one
`(1,4)`, zero `(1,2)`, and one `(2,4)` corner. Substitution gives
`2N_A+N_B <= 2N_A+O(R)` and
`2N_A+N_B <= N_B+O(R)`. Hence each species count is `O(R)`, contradicting
the area lower bound. Trial 3 admits no plane tiling. The adjacent replay
checks the exact finite corner inventory and uniqueness of the third
label; the boundary-density step is the elementary geometric argument
above. This pair is also stopped.
