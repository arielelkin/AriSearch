# Arbitrary convex parallelogram fusions cannot produce an aperiodic pair

Status: proved below and independently hostile-reviewed valid within its restricted scope. Restricted source-family obstruction, not a solution of the main problem. Date: 2026-10-19. [Sol review](../reviews/convex-parallelogram-fusion-periodic-escape-sol-hostile-review-2026-10-19.md).

## Changed construction route and exact first gate

Change from Hat half-kite repartition to a different hierarchical source: a Penrose rhomb tiling, or any tiling whose atoms are parallelograms. Permit each output tile to be an arbitrary finite union of whole source atoms, with no bound on atom count, no fixed connected-subset grid, and arbitrary cross-supertile incidence. Require only that its support is a convex polygon. Ask whether two output congruence classes can form an unmarked aperiodic pair.

**Theorem.** Let A and B each admit a finite dissection into nondegenerate parallelograms, with pairwise disjoint interiors. The dissections need not come from the same source, need not be edge-to-edge, and may use arbitrary orientations and side lengths. Then either {A,B} has no plane tiling, or it admits a doubly periodic monohedral tiling using one member alone. In particular the changed fusion route fails before recognizability or hierarchical forcing is considered.

More generally, the atoms may be arbitrary centrally symmetric convex polygons. The same proof applies.

## 1. Directional cancellation forces central symmetry

Orient every atom boundary counterclockwise. Fix an unoriented line direction d, and fix a positive orientation along that line. For any polygon Q define its directional imbalance I_d(Q) as the sum of signed lengths of its boundary segments parallel to d. Every centrally symmetric atom has I_d=0, since its edges occur in equal opposite pairs.

Subdivide all collinear overlapping atom edges at their endpoints. Each segment in the interior of the dissection then occurs exactly twice, in opposite boundary orientations, and cancels. This remains true at T-junctions: subdivision is only accounting, not an imposed matching rule. Point contacts contribute zero length. Consequently I_d(A)=sum I_d(atom)=0 for every d. The same holds for B.

Remove all collinear redundant vertices from the convex boundary A. In a given unoriented direction there are at most two sides: the supporting sides on opposite sides of the polygon. A genuine side has positive length. Vanishing imbalance therefore requires exactly two sides in that direction, equal in length and oppositely directed. The directions are cyclically ordered; translating the boundary edge list by half its even length sends each vector to its negative. If vertices are p_0,...,p_(2m-1), then p_i+p_(i+m) is independent of i. This common sum gives twice the center. Thus A and B are centrally symmetric, with even numbers of genuine sides, at least four.

This is an exact analytic gate, not a finite enumeration, and has no atom-count cutoff.

## 2. Four- or six-sided output gives an explicit periodic omission

A centrally symmetric quadrilateral is a parallelogram and tiles by the lattice generated by its two adjacent edge vectors.

For a centrally symmetric convex hexagon, write its counterclockwise edge vectors as u,v,w,-u,-v,-w. Translate the first vertex to zero. Its vertices are

    0, u, u+v, u+v+w, v+w, w.

Define lattice vectors a=u+v and b=v+w. Their determinant is

    det(a,b)=det(u,v)+det(u,w)+det(v,w)=area(hexagon)>0.

Opposite sides are paired by translations b, b-a, and -a. Gluing them by these translations gives a torus: the six corners have two equivalence classes, each containing three corners. At each class the angles sum to 2pi, because opposite hexagon angles are equal and its three distinct angles sum to 2pi. Hence the quotient is a nonsingular flat torus. Its universal cover is the Euclidean plane, tiled by copies of the hexagon; its translation lattice is generated by a,b. Equivalently this is the usual elementary translational tiling by a centrally symmetric hexagon. The exact area identity and opposite-side endpoint matches are checked by the accompanying symbolic certificate script.

Therefore if either output has four or six sides, that member alone yields an admitted doubly periodic tiling. Reflections and arbitrary contacts cannot remove that tiling.

## 3. If both outputs have at least eight sides, no plane tiling exists

We supply the boundary argument including partial-edge contacts. A finite palette of compact polygons with nonempty interiors has constants D,r>0 so every tile has diameter at most D and contains a disk of radius r. Inscribed disks in distinct tile interiors are disjoint. Hence only finitely many tiles meet a bounded region; their number near a square boundary of length O(R) is O(R), by packing their inscribed disks into a boundary neighborhood of fixed width D+r.

Clip a putative plane tiling to a generic square S_R of side R. Generic means its four corners avoid tiling edges and its sides avoid tiling vertices and do not contain tiling edge segments; such choices exist since the tiling is locally finite and globally countable. Positive-area intersections form a finite convex dissection of S_R. Let F_i count tiles wholly inside the square and F_b count boundary-cut tiles. Each full tile has at least eight genuine corners. Each clipped tile has at least three. We have F_b=O(R), while F_i=Omega(R^2), since each tile has area bounded above and the boundary-cut tiles occupy only an O(R) area neighborhood.

For completeness, any finite convex dissection of a convex p-gon obeys

    sum_faces(6-n_f)=6-p+V_b+V_T+2X >= 6-p.

Here graph vertices include every genuine face corner; V_b counts extra vertices on the outer boundary, V_T counts interior T-junctions with one straight face sector, V_f counts other interior vertices. Let c(v) count genuine face corners. The minimum c is 1 at an outer polygon corner, 2 at a V_b or V_T vertex, and 3 at a V_f vertex. Let X be total excess over these minima. Thus

    N=sum n_f=p+2V_b+2V_T+3V_f+X.

Vertex degrees are c+1 at outer corners, V_b and V_T vertices, and c at V_f vertices. Therefore 2E=N+p+V_b+V_T. Euler V-E+F=1 with V=p+V_b+V_T+V_f yields the displayed identity after elimination. All collinear subdivisions were counted as graph vertices, so T-junctions are explicitly included.

Apply it with p=4. The left side is at most -2F_i+3F_b, which tends to minus infinity as R grows. The right side is at least 2. Contradiction. This establishes the no-tiling alternative without an edge-to-edge hypothesis, without assuming both species occur, and without a positive frequency assumption.

Combining sections 1–3 proves the theorem.

**Corollary (whole centrally symmetric class).** For any pair of centrally
symmetric convex polygons, with no fusion hypothesis, either the pair has no
plane tiling or one member admits a doubly periodic monohedral tiling.
Indeed sections 2–3 use only central symmetry, not the dissection. This is
still a proper restricted class and does not settle the main problem.

## 5. Scope, limitations, and changed next mechanism

The theorem applies to unions of whole parallelogram atoms, even when fusions cross any number of source supertiles. It does not apply to arbitrary cuts through source atoms, unions of Penrose triangles, Hat kites, Spectres, or dual cells unless those output cells themselves have centrally symmetric dissections. Retaining source markings cannot rescue the unmarked output definition. Arbitrary boundary displacement or smoothing is outside the theorem because the directional cancellation identity need not survive.

An actual next positive route must break the directional balance: for example, fuse an explicitly selected Penrose triangle inventory with unbalanced boundary direction counts, or use genuinely moved cross-source cut endpoints. That requires an exact plane-covering two-class construction plus an independent monohedral escape audit; none is supplied here. No enlarged whole- or half-kite grid was searched. The main objective remains unresolved.
