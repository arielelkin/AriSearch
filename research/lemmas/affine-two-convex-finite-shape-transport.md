# Finite shape permutation does not rescue anisotropic conjugation

Date: 2026-10-13. Status: adversarially reviewed VALID after scalar-power scope correction. Independent review: `research/reviews/affine-two-convex-finite-shape-transport-hostile-review.md`.

## Exact assumptions and theorem

Let P be a finite set of convex polygons with nonempty interiors and M an invertible expanding real 2 by 2 matrix that is not a Euclidean similarity. Suppose each MP has a finite interior-disjoint dissection into copies RQ+t, Q in P, R orthogonal. Instead of requiring all conjugated placement maps to be orthogonal, assume the following weaker **finite transported-shape condition**:

For every placed child pair (R,Q), all polygons (M^n R M^(-n))Q, n>=0, belong, up to Euclidean congruence, to a finite set of polygons. This set may contain additional species and need not equal P. Translations in choosing origins do not affect this condition.

Then either M has a positive scalar power, or every placement orientation R commutes with M. In the latter case all parent and child boundary directions form a finite M-invariant set. If M has real eigenvalues of unequal moduli, every polygon of P is a parallelogram along its two eigenlines, and each has a monohedral periodic tiling. Jordan matrices and complex matrices with irrational eigenargument cannot satisfy the assumptions.

Thus finite species permutation under normalized conjugation does not rescue genuinely anisotropic stationary transport. Arbitrary orientation-dependent alternate dissections that do not satisfy the explicitly stated transported-shape condition remain outside this theorem.

## Common preliminary facts

Write T_n=M^n R M^(-n) for one fixed placed child (R,Q). Finite congruence classes imply that the squared lengths |T_n v|^2 of every vector v joining two vertices of Q belong to a finite set: any linear image sends vertices to vertices, and congruence preserves their pairwise distances. The operators T_n are uniformly bounded: choose two linearly independent such vectors; their images have bounded lengths, and they form a fixed basis.

## Real eigenvalues of unequal moduli

Choose eigenvectors e1,e2 and order the eigenvalues so |lambda1|>|lambda2|. In that eigenbasis write R=((a,b),(c,d)), and r=lambda1/lambda2. Then

    T_n=((a,b r^n),(c r^(-n),d)).

Uniform boundedness forces b=0. Choose a vertex-difference vector v=x e1+y e2 with x nonzero. Its squared transformed length is

    |a x e1+d y e2+c x r^(-n)e2|^2
      = C0+C1 r^(-n)+c^2 x^2 |e2|^2 r^(-2n).

This expression takes finitely many values while r^(-n) gives infinitely many distinct real numbers. A nonconstant polynomial of degree at most two has at most two preimages per value, so it must be constant. Its quadratic coefficient is zero, forcing c=0. Hence R is diagonal in the eigenbasis and commutes with M. This proof permits negative eigenvalues and oblique eigenvectors.

## Real Jordan case

After scalar normalization M=I+N, N^2=0, N nonzero. Conjugation gives

    T_n=(I+nN)R(I-nN)=R+n(NR-RN)-n^2 NRN.

Uniform boundedness of this matrix polynomial forces it to be constant. In particular NR=RN and R commutes with M.

## Complex eigenvalues with irrational eigenargument

Write M=rho E Rot(theta) E^(-1). If theta/pi is irrational, the conjugates T_n are dense in the continuous conjugation curve

    T(t)=E Rot(t) E^(-1) R E Rot(-t) E^(-1).

For every vertex-difference vector v of Q, the continuous function |T(t)v|^2 has image contained in the finite set of squared vertex distances of the finite transported species: this follows first on the dense orbit n theta and then by closedness of that finite set. A continuous real function on the circle with finite image is constant. At t=0, R is orthogonal, so |T(t)v|=|v| for all t.

Take three noncollinear vertices of Q and their difference vectors v,w,v-w. Preservation of these three squared norms implies preservation of the Gram matrix of the basis v,w. Thus T(t) is orthogonal for all t. Also M T(t) M^(-1)=T(t+theta) is orthogonal, so each T(t) commutes with the positive matrix S=M^T M. Since M is not a similarity, S has distinct eigenvalues, and its orthogonal centralizer has exactly four elements. Continuity therefore forces T(t)=R for all t. In particular MR=RM.

If theta/pi is rational, some positive power of M is scalar. A diagonalizable real matrix with equal-modulus eigenvalues likewise has a scalar square (the scalar matrix itself is already a similarity). These are the alternatives not excluded.

## Boundary directions and periodic escape

Every orthogonal matrix commuting with a nonsimilar M commutes with S=M^T M and therefore belongs to its four-element orthogonal centralizer. Let H be the group generated by all placed orientations; it has at most four elements and every member commutes with M. If D0 is the finite set of native unoriented prototile side directions, D=H D0 is finite. Straight parent boundary sides are covered by child sides even when the dissection has partial contacts. Hence M D0 is a subset of D, and commutation implies M D is a subset of D. Invertibility gives M D=D.

For real unequal-modulus eigenvalues the only periodic projective lines are the two eigenlines. Convexity then makes every prototile a parallelogram, giving its edge-vector translation lattice as an admitted periodic tiling. A real Jordan matrix has only one periodic projective line, impossible for a polygon with interior. An irrational complex rotation conjugate has none, also impossible. These conclusions concern the explicit substitution architecture, not arbitrary pairs of convex tiles.

## Dependencies and review gate

Elementary real linear algebra, finite polygon vertex distances and polygon boundary covering suffice. This extends `affine-two-convex-iterable-obstruction.md` by allowing finite congruence-class permutation, but does not infer finite transported-shape closure from arbitrary plane tilability or from mere existence of unrelated dissections of MP. The finite scalar-power regime remains a matrix exception. Scalar powers alone do not provide an iterated dissection: additional transported congruence species may have no specified substitution rules. An ordinary similarity substitution follows only if actual iterability is separately established, as in the stronger equivariant theorem.
