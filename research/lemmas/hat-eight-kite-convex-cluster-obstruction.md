# The fixed hat's convex whole-kite clusters cannot force aperiodicity

Date: 2026-10-12. **Adversarially reviewed VALID:**
[independent hostile review](../reviews/hat-eight-kite-convex-cluster-review.md).

## Statement and exact scope

Let K_0,...,K_7 be the eight standard Laves-grid kites of the reference
Tile(1,sqrt3) hat, in the exact order and coordinates retained in
`research/certificates/hat-eight-kite-convex-cluster-gate.json`.
Let S be any finite nonempty collection of shapes, each congruent to the
convex union of a nonempty subset of these eight cells. Then S admits a
periodic tiling using one member alone. In particular S is not aperiodic.

There are sixteen such convex subsets: eight quadrilaterals, one triangle
and seven pentagons. The only partition of the reference hat into subsets
with at least five intrinsic corners is four congruent pentagons, so it
does not even produce two final congruence classes.

This theorem permits reflections and unrestricted contacts. It concerns
whole-cell clusters contained within one fixed reference hat, not cuts
through cells, cross-hat clusters, nonstandard cells, or all Tile(a,b).

## Definitions and source attribution

The positive square root r of3 defines the coordinate field Q(r). The
certificate represents each coordinate as two rational coefficients of
1,r. It gives the entire thirteen-vertex reference outline and all eight
four-vertex cells. Thus the finite object does not depend on a picture or
an unavailable patch file.

The hat's description as eight kites of the [3.4.6.4] Laves tiling and its
aperiodicity are primary-source facts from Smith, Myers, Kaplan and
Goodman-Strauss, [An aperiodic monotile](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.10798v3),
Theorem1.1. No source aperiodicity theorem is needed for the obstruction:
the periodic escape belongs to the final convex shapes themselves.

## Exact finite classification

Each nonempty cluster is one of255 subsets. Its cells have disjoint
interiors. Its union is compact and contained in its convex hull. It equals
that hull precisely when the hull's area equals the sum of cell areas:
if there were a missing point in the hull's interior, closedness of the
union would give a missing open neighbourhood of positive area; boundary
points follow by closure. Exact supporting lines and area calculations
therefore decide convexity without a numerical tolerance.

The complete result, using mask=sum_(i included)2^i, is:

* singleton masks1,2,4,8,16,32,64,128: the standard kite;
* mask168: an equilateral triangle;
* masks6,9,12,48,192: congruent unions of two consecutive kites;
* masks13,14: congruent unions of three consecutive kites.

The last two groups each have five intrinsic corners. There are no other
convex subsets. The independent checker validates all255 cases by its own
edge-cancelled boundaries and supporting-line tests. It additionally checks
all28 pairwise kite intersections have zero area and that cancelling all
internal edges recovers the exact source outline. Thus the computation
does not assume the source producer's area or hull decisions.

Enumerating partitions using the seven pentagonal masks gives only
{6,9,48,192}. Its four pieces are congruent. This partition statement is
secondary: the periodic escapes below already kill every allowed shape.

## Periodic monohedral escapes

Let H be the regular hexagon of radius2 centred at the origin. A
representative two-kite pentagon is

    P=[(0,0),(3/2,-r/2),(2,0),(1,r),(0,r)].

Rotations of P about0 by0,120,240 degrees partition H. A representative
three-kite pentagon is

    Q=[(0,-r),(1,-r),(2,0),(1,r),(0,r)].

It is the right half of H; Q and its180-degree rotation partition H.
The standard kite itself gives the usual six-cell partition of H.
For each of these shapes, repeat its fixed dissection of H over the
hexagon lattice generated by (3,r) and (0,2r). This gives a periodic
monohedral tiling by that shape. The finite partitions have been
independently checked by exact boundary cancellation and intersection area.

Every triangle tiles periodically: the triangle and its half-turn about
one side's midpoint form a parallelogram, whose lattice translates tile.
These constructions exhaust every convex cluster class. Choose any member
of S and use its corresponding monohedral tiling. The definition permits
omitting all other members, proving the theorem. QED.
