# Saturated zigzags force a period for the resonant heptagon

**Theorem.** Let P be a unit regular pentagon. Let Q be any fixed strictly convex heptagon with cyclic angle word `AABBBBC`, where `A=144°`, `B=120°`, and `C=132°`. If copies of P and Q tile the Euclidean plane under arbitrary isometries and unrestricted contacts, then the pair admits a tiling with a nonzero translation. No metric restriction beyond strict convexity and the stated angles is imposed. The original tiling need not itself be periodic.

**Proof.** The [complementary-angle necessary theorem](complementary-angle-flow-necessary-theorem-2026-10-19.md) shows that all contacts are full-edge and each vertex is PPA, PBC, or BBB. In a square of side R, write p_R,q_R for tile counts and let t(Q) count B corners of one Q assigned BBB. Its global incidences give `p_R=q_R+O(R+1)` and `Σt(Q)=3q_R+O(R+1)`. Locally `t(Q)∈{1,3}`. Each Q has exactly one AA side and that side faces P. A P can face at most one AA side, because such a side is flanked by two PP sides and a regular pentagon cannot have consecutive PP sides. Counting AA contacts gives `Σa(P)=q_R+O(R+1)`, where a(P) is 1 if P faces an AA side and 0 otherwise. Thus both nonnegative deficits `3−t(Q)` and `1−a(P)` total O(R+1) in every square, uniformly over translations.

For any L, a sufficiently large square has a center at distance more than L plus a fixed tile diameter from every defective tile anchor: O(R) fixed-radius excluded disks cannot cover area Ω(R²). Translate a defect-free radius-L neighborhood to the origin and let L tend to infinity. Compactness of isometric placements of two fixed positive-area, bounded-diameter polygons gives a whole-plane limit tiling. Full-edge incidences persist under this limit, so every tile is defect-free. This constructs another admitted tiling in which every Q has t=3 and every P faces exactly one AA side.

In the saturated tiling, a Q has three PQ sides, namely its AA side and the two adjacent sides, and four consecutive QQ sides. Pair each Q to the unique P across its AA side. Every P has two PP sides adjacent to its AA-facing side, plus three PQ sides; the AA pairing is a bijection. Across a PP edge, the two P tiles must select opposite endpoints for their AA sides. If they selected the same endpoint, the single Q at that PPA vertex would need two distinct AA sides, while its word has only one. Hence the PP adjacency graph has degree two and its center path alternates turns +36° and −36°. With pentagon apothem `r=1/(2tan36°)`, successive center steps have length 2r and alternate between two vectors of mutual angle 36°. Their two-step sum v has length `4r cos18°>0`. A component is therefore a doubly infinite proper zigzag, and translation by v maps each P to the P two steps ahead, preserving the distinguished AA side.

The Q paired to P_n also faces P_(n−1) and P_(n+1) along its two AA-adjacent sides, by the PPA stars at the AA endpoints. The unique PBC corner of P_n joins Q_(n−1) and Q_(n+1). On a fixed AA side and half-plane there are only two placements of Q, exchanged by reflection; they interchange which adjacent side has far-end label B and which has label C. Let F_j be the B/C label on Q_j's side toward P_(j+1), and H_j the label toward P_(j−1). Always H_j is the complement of F_j. The PBC corner of P_n requires F_(n−1) and H_(n+1) complementary, hence `F_(n−1)=F_(n+1)`. The Q choice therefore repeats every two P tiles, so `Q_(n+2)=Q_n+v`. Each decorated zigzag of P and its paired Q is invariant under v.

Center segments of distinct PP zigzags lie within disjoint pentagon interiors except at their own shared side midpoints, so distinct paths cannot meet. Each is a proper graph at bounded distance from its v-axis. Two such paths with nonparallel axes would cross, since the ends of one lie on opposite sides of the other. Thus all axes are parallel. Every two-step vector has the same nonzero length, so their vectors are ±v for one common v. Each decorated zigzag, hence their union covering all P and Q, is invariant under translation by v. ∎

It proves a single nonzero period for a tiling obtained by purification; a second independent period is not required to exclude aperiodicity.
