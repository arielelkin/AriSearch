# Periodic tilings throughout the regular-pentagon / 144–126 heptagon family

**Theorem (restricted family).** Let `w=exp(2*pi*i/20)`, `t=w+w^-1=2cos(18°)`, and `k=2cos(72°)`. Let `P` have vertices `p_i=sum_{j<i}w^(4j)` for `i=0,...,4`. For any real `h>-t`, let `Q(h)` have vertices `q_i=sum_{j<i}e_j`, `i=0,...,6`, where

`(e_0,...,e_6)=(1,(t+h)w^3,w^6,(t+kh)w^9,w^12,(t+h)w^15,w^18)`.

Then `P` and `Q(h)` are distinct strictly convex polygons, and their unmarked pair admits a doubly periodic tiling of the Euclidean plane. Hence no member of this exact one-parameter pair family is aperiodic under the definitions of [the research problem](../../research-problem.md).

The statement includes every real `h>-t`, including transcendental values. It asserts existence of a periodic tiling; it does not classify all tilings of the pair or any other family.

## Exact four-face construction

Take four representatives, with counterclockwise native vertex orders,

`F0=P`, `F1=1-P`, `F2=1-w^6 Q(h)`, `F3=w^6 Q(h)`.

Translate them by the lattice generated by

`L1=w*h+2w^4+w^2+2`,

`L2=(w^7-w^5+w^3-w)*h+3w^6-w^4+w^2-3`.

The full list of twelve reversed edge pairings, their native dart indices and integer lattice shifts is in the [self-contained derivation](../searches/regular-pentagon-heptagon-periodic-transfer-sol-2026-10-19.md). Its [symbolic Sage replay](../searches/regular-pentagon-heptagon-periodic-transfer-sol-2026-10-19.sage) proves both endpoint identities for every pair as polynomials over `Q(w)[h]`. The [independent hostile checker](../reviews/regular-pentagon-heptagon-periodic-transfer-hostile-sol-2026-10-19.sage) rebuilds them over ordered exact real polynomials `AA[x]`, where `x=h+t`.

## Proof of geometric admissibility and coverage

The exact closure identities for `Q` are

`1+w^6+w^12+w^18+t(w^3+w^9+w^15)=0` and `w^3+k*w^9+w^15=0`.

For `x=h+t>0`, the seven edge lengths are `1,x,1,(1-k)t+kx,1,x,1`; all are positive. Their successive turns give one 144° and six 126° interior angles. Strict convexity, including absence of a nonlocal self-crossing, follows from all 35 edge/other-vertex signed cross products being positive. The producer certificate and independent checker establish this as a coefficientwise positive-polynomial result in `x>0`. The analogous 15 tests hold for `P`.

The paired-edge identities make the four abstract polygons a connected closed oriented surface `X`; every edge is paired once. Its eight vertex links are single three-cycles: two of type `P,P,Q(144°)` and six of type `P,Q(126°),Q(126°)`. Every cycle has total angle 360° and zero endpoint holonomy. The edge identities map `X` continuously to the flat torus `T=R²/(ZL1+ZL2)`. The sectors along each edge lie on opposite sides, and the consistently oriented positive vertex sectors make one complete 360° turn. Thus this map is a local Euclidean isometry everywhere, including at vertices; it does not presume that the displayed plane representatives are nonoverlapping.

Writing `a=sin(36°)>0`, exact algebra gives the oriented lattice area

`cross(L1,L2)=a*x²+(4+k)*x+2t+4a>0`.

The exact shoelace identity gives `cross(L1,L2)=2 area(P)+2 area(Q(h))=area(X)`. Therefore `T` is a genuine rank-two flat torus. Compactness and connectedness make the local isometry `X->T` a finite surjective covering. Its degree equals `area(X)/area(T)=1`, so it is a homeomorphism. Lifting to the plane proves that all lattice translates of `F0,...,F3` cover the plane with pairwise disjoint interiors. Both lattice generators are nonzero translational periods.

This argument is valid separately for each `h>-t`, with no uniform injectivity-radius assertion at the excluded endpoint. The endpoint `h=-t` has zero-length edges and is outside the strictly convex heptagon family. Reflections and partial-edge contacts are allowed in the problem; a single permitted periodic tiling already falsifies aperiodicity, so no exhaustive reflection or contact classification is needed.
