# Convex-cluster fusion lemma

**Status:** The opening conditional fusion theorem has a
[fresh independent VALID rereview](../reviews/convex-cluster-fusion-basic-fresh-rereview.md).
Ancillary trivalent/cluster statements retain their separate review gates. The
exact-patch, unrestricted-isometry, tile-level-partition, and distinct-class
hypotheses required by that review are now stated here. See [research/reviews/convex-cluster-fusion-review.md](../reviews/convex-cluster-fusion-review.md).

## Statement

Let \(S\) be a finite set of compact tiles under unrestricted Euclidean
isometries (including reflections) and unrestricted contacts, such that:

1. at least one \(S\)-tiling of the plane exists; and
2. no \(S\)-tiling has a nonzero translational symmetry.

Let \(C=\{T_1,\ldots,T_k\}\) be a finite legal \(S\)-patch of closed tiles,
with pairwise disjoint interiors, whose union is exactly a compact convex polygon
\(M\).  Let \(B\in S\).  Suppose some \(S\)-tiling \(\mathcal T_0\) can be
partitioned at the tile level into patches congruent to \(C\) and singleton
copies of \(B\), covering every tile occurrence.
Then \(\{M,B\}\) is an aperiodic protoset, provided that \(M\) and \(B\) are
distinct congruence classes.

Here congruence and tilings permit reflections, and a tiling may use only one
prototile, exactly as in [research-problem.md](../../research-problem.md).

## Proof

Replacing every patch congruent to \(C\) in \(\mathcal T_0\) by its union
produces an \(\{M,B\}\)-tiling.  This proves existence.

Suppose, for a contradiction, that an \(\{M,B\}\)-tiling \(\mathcal U\) has
a nonzero translation symmetry \(t\).  Translation by \(t\) acts freely on
the tiles of \(\mathcal U\): a nonempty compact set cannot equal a nonzero
translate of itself, since iteration would make it unbounded.  Choose one
tile from each orbit of the action generated by \(t\).  For every chosen
copy of \(M\), choose any isometry carrying the abstract polygon \(M\) onto
that copy and insert the images of the fixed patch pieces
\(T_1,\ldots,T_k\).  Insert the corresponding translated dissection in every
other \(M\)-tile in its orbit.  Leave every \(B\)-tile undivided.

Each inserted patch covers its former \(M\)-tile and has pairwise
interior-disjoint pieces.  Distinct former tiles already had disjoint
interiors, so all inserted pieces together form an \(S\)-tiling.  The choices
were transported along every \(t\)-orbit, hence translation by \(t\) maps
this dissected tiling to itself.  This contradicts hypothesis 2.  Therefore
every \(\{M,B\}\)-tiling is nonperiodic.  Together with existence and the
distinctness proviso, this proves the claim. \(\square\)

## Scope and non-consequences

* No recognizability or unique-dissection hypothesis is needed.  The proof
  needs only one fixed dissection per translation orbit of a hypothetical
  periodic tiling.
* The lemma is conditional on an aperiodic source set under the *same*
  convention about reflections and unrestricted contacts.
* It does not produce a suitable convex cluster.  One must still prove that
  a source tiling partitions globally into congruent copies of a particular
  finite patch and residual singleton tiles.
* It works for a single translational period; no rank-two lattice is assumed.

## Constructive application obligation

For the classical Ammann recomposition into two pentagons and one hexagon,
the most economical finite search is for a translation- or
substitution-compatible perfect matching between two tile classes along a
shared edge.  Their union is convex exactly when the sums of the two incident
interior angles at both endpoints of the deleted seam are at most \(\pi\).
Before enumerating geometry, an exact frequency test is necessary: pairwise
matching of classes \(i,j\) requires equal frequencies \(f_i=f_j\).  More
generally, if a cluster has integer content vector \(u\) and tile class
\(r\) remains singleton, the source frequency vector must have the form
\(f=\lambda u+\mu e_r\), with \(\lambda,\mu>0\).

## Trivalent-vertex obstruction

Assume source polygons are written without redundant collinear vertices, so
every interior angle is strictly less than \(\pi\).

### Lemma

In a locally finite edge-to-edge tiling, let three tile sectors meet at a
vertex \(v\).  A convex finite cluster cannot contain exactly two of those
three incident tiles while omitting the third.  In particular, the union of
two tiles sharing a full edge is nonconvex if either endpoint of that edge is
3-valent.

### Proof

Let the three incident angles be \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\), with the cluster
containing the first two sectors.  The tiling condition gives
\(\alpha+\beta+\gamma=2\pi\), while the omitted convex polygon has
\(\gamma<\pi\).  Hence the cluster occupies a reflex aperture
\(\alpha+\beta=2\pi-\gamma>\pi\) at \(v\).  Local finiteness ensures that no
nonincident cluster tile enters a sufficiently small punctured neighbourhood
of \(v\).  The tangent cone of a convex set at a boundary point is convex and
has aperture at most \(\pi\), so the cluster is not convex.  For two tiles
sharing an edge, apply the same argument at either 3-valent endpoint.
\(\square\)

Thus, at each trivalent vertex, the cluster labels on the three incident
tiles must be either all equal or all distinct, never exactly two equal.  This
is a local propagation rule for any exact cluster search; it does not by
itself rule out larger convex clusters or a two-prototile fusion.

## Decorated periodic-lift variant

The fusion theorem above deliberately assumes an **unmarked** strongly
aperiodic source.  For a decorated source the correct replacement is the
following stronger obligation.

### Lemma

Let \(X\) be a nonempty tiling space of finitely many decorated compact tiles
with no nonzero translationally invariant member.  Let \(A,B\) be distinct
unmarked compact tiles, each equipped with allowed finite decorated
\(X\)-patches whose support is exactly that tile.  Suppose:

1. some member of \(X\) partitions into these \(A\)- and \(B\)-supported
   patches; and
2. for every nonzero \(t\) and every \(t\)-invariant unmarked
   \(\{A,B\}\)-tiling, allowed patches can be selected constantly on
   \(t\)-orbits so that their decorations match across every macro-boundary
   and their union belongs to \(X\).

Then \(\{A,B\}\) is aperiodic.

### Proof

The first hypothesis gives a target tiling after forgetting decorations.  If
a target tiling had period \(t\ne0\), the second hypothesis would lift it
equivariantly to a member of \(X\) with the same period, contradicting the
definition of \(X\). \(\square\)

The second condition is the **periodic-lift obligation**.  It must cover
reflections and partial-edge contacts unless those possibilities have already
been excluded.  Existence of one decorated partition does not imply it.

## No finite convex multitile cluster in a trivalent tiling

### Theorem

In a connected, locally finite, edge-to-edge, everywhere-3-valent plane
tiling by genuine convex polygons (with no redundant straight corners), the
union of a finite tile patch is convex only when the patch consists of a
single tile.

### Proof

If the convex union \(U\) contained more than one tile, its tile
edge-adjacency graph would contain an edge: the open convex set
\(\operatorname{int}U\) is connected, and a path through the finite cell
decomposition cannot pass between tile interiors using vertex contacts only.
Choose two included tiles sharing an edge.  At either endpoint, the unique
third incident tile must also be included by the trivalent-vertex obstruction.
In the planar dual cellulation, every primal trivalent vertex is a triangular
dual face.  Thus whenever both endpoints of a dual edge belong to the
cluster, the third vertex of each of the two incident triangular dual faces
also belongs to it.

Completing the initial dual edge adds its two incident triangular faces.
Whenever a triangular face has been added, all endpoints of each of its edges
are included, so the rule adds the triangle on the other side of every edge.
The face-adjacency graph of the dual triangulation is connected: a path in
the plane in general position between interiors of two primal-vertex dual
faces crosses finitely many dual edges.  Therefore every dual face and vertex
is eventually forced.  Closure of one included adjacency would make the
cluster contain every tile, contradicting finiteness.
\(\square\)

This rules out obtaining finite convex macrotiles by clustering *any*
everywhere-trivalent edge-to-edge convex source tiling.  In particular the
all-obtuse edge-to-edge mechanism cannot simultaneously supply a convex
fusion construction.  The theorem does not apply to source tilings with
T-junctions, higher-valence vertices, or non-edge-to-edge contacts.
