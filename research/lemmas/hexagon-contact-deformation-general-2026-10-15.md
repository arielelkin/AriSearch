# General hexagon contact-deformation obstruction

Date:2026-10-15. Status: independently hostile-reviewed VALID after the explicit
monohedral-branch repair;
[review](../reviews/hexagon-contact-deformation-general-hostile-2026-10-15.md). This is a restricted necessary condition for an aperiodic pair,
not a resolution of research-problem.md.

## Statement and definitions

Let A,B be two distinct genuine convex hexagons. Let T be any full-side,
everywhere-trivalent tiling by these polygons. Choose for each occurrence
an indexed isometry from its reference polygon; reference symmetries need
not make that indexing canonical. Let E be the equivalence relation on the
twelve native side slots generated by ALL full seams occurring in T.
Every E-class C has one common original positive side length x_C.

Write u_Ai,u_Bj for the native directed unit side vectors, in positive
boundary order, and let M:R^E -> R^4 be the closure map

    Mv=(sum_i v_[Ai] u_Ai, sum_j v_[Bj] u_Bj).

For each pair i!=k of originally equal A side lengths, define the linear
functional d_ik(v)=v_[Ai]-v_[Ak]. Suppose NONE of these functionals belongs
to the row space of M. Then the original unmarked pair admits a
plane tiling with a nonzero translation. The same statement holds on
interchanging A and B. If A has no equal side pair, the hypothesis is empty.

Consequently, a hypothetical aperiodic pair must, for each shape and each
indexed defect-free tiling, have at least one repeated native pair whose
difference functional belongs to row(M). The pair is allowed to depend on
the tiling and its indexing. If two slots are E-equivalent their difference
functional is zero and automatically belongs to that row space. Thus this
criterion does not discard contact identifications that the tiling needs.

## 1. Full-side periodic witnesses from a MIXED changed source

Assume in this section that the changed pair has a MIXED full-side
trivalent source. If B has at most three numerical lengths, apply the
MIXED portion of the reviewed
[three-sharing proof](hexagon-six-distinct-three-shared-periodicity-2026-10-15.md)
directly: its AAB corona rule gives centrally symmetric B, with a lattice
full-side tiling, or its mixed deterministic tiling, already full-side and
doubly periodic. This argument does not pass to a B-only limit and does
not require B to fail monohedral tileability.

Now suppose B has at least four distinct lengths. If B tiles alone, the
imported monohedral classification puts it in Reinhardt H1 or H2: H3 has
three equal side pairs and thus at most three distinct lengths. H1 and H2
have full-side finite torus witnesses (explicitly checked in the fresh
review of this extension). Their native angle and side conditions suffice
for those witnesses. Hence in this branch there is a full-side periodic
changed tiling. If B does not tile alone, continue as follows.

The reviewed [one-distinct-sided theorem](hexagon-six-distinct-unrestricted-periodic-escape-2026-10-15.md)
provides a full-side periodic witness in every branch except that its
[private-AA-chain argument](periodic-private-aa-chain-escape-2026-10-15.md)
explicitly requires only unrestricted coverage in the final strip filling.
Here is the needed strengthening of that branch.

Keep its fixed T-periodic A carrier, separator bands, finite exact B
candidate placements, and finite selection alphabet. Add these local
conditions to its finite-window constraints:

1. Every selected B native edge has a mate consisting of one WHOLE native
   edge of a selected B or of the fixed A carrier, with opposite interiors.
2. Every fixed A nonprivate edge has an existing whole-edge fixed-A mate
   or a selected whole-edge B mate with opposite interiors.

The original defect-free filling satisfies both conditions. Every possible
mate is at bounded distance from the edge, since the two tile diameters
are bounded. In the finite candidate alphabet, these are finite-window
conditions with a uniform memory bound. There are uniformly finitely many
fixed A edges per slab, by inradius packing. Coverage and no-overlap are
still imposed as in the original proof. The enlarged one-dimensional
shift therefore contains the original bi-infinite sequence. A directed
graph cycle supplies a periodic filling with the same uniform cycle bound
construction, increasing that bound if the memory was increased.

These conditions can be imposed separately in each band. A private A
edge already has its fixed AA mate. Every nonprivate A edge, INCLUDING its
endpoints, is disjoint from all separator curves: those curves lie in A
interiors or private-AA-edge interiors and avoid native vertices. The
connected nonprivate edge therefore lies in one open band. Every B tile
also avoids the separator curves and belongs to one band. Thus each edge
condition involving B belongs to exactly one band; independent band
fillings cannot break an edge condition across a separator. Existing fixed
AA edges are checked as fixed geometry. Synchronizing the bounded band
periods as before yields a full-side periodic plane tiling.

No new existence or finite-model hypothesis was inserted: the original
full-side tiling witnesses nonemptiness of every enlarged band shift.
The other branches of the synthesis already give full-side tori or
full-side deterministic tilings. The monohedral branches were handled separately above. Hence for a mixed
source the strengthened synthesis provides a full-side witness with at
least one nonzero translation. No claim that every H3 monohedral witness
is full-side is needed.

## 2. Transfer of any indexed full-side periodic plane complex

Consider two finite sets of indexed convex polygons with the same native
angles and side directions before and after changing lengths. Suppose
there is a full-side periodic tiling by the changed polygons, and every
changed matched-length equality also holds for the corresponding original
native sides. Its abstract face complex can be restored to the original
polygon metrics: every seam is still an equal full-side gluing, and every
vertex has the same total angle2pi.

This metric plane is complete. Triangulate each native polygon from its
vertex average to its vertices. Corresponding original/changed triangles
are related by invertible affine maps. There are only finitely many
triangles in the templates, so a uniform positive bilipschitz bound applies
to all face maps. On a shared edge, both maps are the same affine map of
the two endpoints, so the maps agree globally. They give a bilipschitz
homeomorphism of the changed plane to the restored intrinsic metric
plane. Completeness follows. The topological plane is locally flat, so
its complete simply connected flat metric develops isometrically to the
Euclidean plane. Thus the restored faces form a valid full-side tiling.

Let tau be one nonzero translation of the changed tiling. Index its tiles
tau-equivariantly: choose an index for one representative of every tau
orbit and transport it by every integer power. Such an orbit has no
finite cycle because a nonzero translation cannot preserve a compact
tile. Distinct polygon types stay distinct, so tau preserves type. This
choice handles all finite reference symmetries. The induced automorphism
of the abstract plane preserves the restored indexed face metrics. It
is therefore a global isometry of the restored plane. It preserves
orientation and has no fixed point, since these are topological properties
of the same underlying map tau. It is nonidentity and hence a nonzero
Euclidean translation: every nonidentity orientation-preserving Euclidean
isometry other than a translation is a rotation with a fixed point.

This argument uses no finite quotient and no bound on its period. One
rank-one full-side witness suffices.

## 3. The simultaneous separating deformation

If T uses only B, original B already has an unrestricted monohedral
periodic escape by the reviewed imported theorem, and the conclusion
holds without deformation. T cannot use only A when A already has six
distinct lengths. If A has repeats and T uses only A, original A likewise
has a monohedral periodic escape. Therefore restrict to mixed T.

For each repeated pair, d_ik not in row(M) means that d_ik is nonzero on
ker(M), by finite-dimensional real linear algebra. Each zero set
ker(M) intersect ker(d_ik) is a proper linear subspace of ker(M). A finite
union of proper subspaces cannot cover a real vector space. Choose one
v in ker(M) outside all of them. It simultaneously separates ALL equal
native A pairs. If there are no repeated pairs use v=0 and the original
one-distinct-sided theorem directly.

For sufficiently small nonzero epsilon set x_C(epsilon)=x_C+epsilon v_C.
Both polygons close because Mx=Mv=0. Positivity and all strict convexity
support inequalities persist by continuity. Their native side directions
and angles do not change. Originally unequal side lengths remain unequal:
there are only finitely many pairs among the twelve slots, each with
nonzero original difference, and epsilon can avoid every possible new
collision. Originally equal A pairs split by the choice of v. Thus the
changed A has six distinct lengths; the changed B has arbitrary allowed
multiplicities. The two shapes remain noncongruent for sufficiently small
epsilon. To see this, any congruence must match one of finitely many
cyclic or reversed vertex correspondences; a sequence of such congruences
approaching epsilon=0 would, after fixing one correspondence, make the
original shapes congruent, contrary to the hypothesis.

Giving the original abstract tiling T its changed metrics constructs a
complete flat plane by the same finite-template argument of Section2.
All seam lengths match because each seam remains inside one E-class.
All vertex angles remain2pi. Hence the changed pair tiles the plane.
The changed source is still mixed. Apply Section1 to obtain
ONE full-side periodic changed tiling. Every changed native side equality
also held originally, by our no-new-collision choice of epsilon. Section2
restores this witness to the original polygons and transfers its nonzero
translation. This proves the statement.

## Exact linear certificate and scope

For a fixed repeated pair, failure to separate it is exactly

    rank(M with row d_ik appended)=rank(M).

The failure of simultaneous separation is the existence of AT LEAST ONE
repeated pair with this rank equality; it does not require all repeated
pairs to be individually rigid. For exact algebraic coordinates, all rows
can be represented in the field adjoining native side-length square
roots. The theorem also permits arbitrary real polygon coordinates; no
floating-point rank decision is justified.

Dependencies: the reviewed defect-free limit theorem supplies T from an
unrestricted tileable hexagon pair, unless its limit is monohedral, which
already has a periodic escape. The strengthened synthesis and transfer
above concern existence of a new unmarked tiling; no matching restrictions
are imposed on the original problem. No result for pentagons follows.
The earlier [A5/B5 torus-transfer lemma](hexagon-fixed-angle-contact-deformation-2026-10-15.md)
is a reviewed special case requiring no strip strengthening. This general
extension has passed its own fresh hostile audit, linked above.
