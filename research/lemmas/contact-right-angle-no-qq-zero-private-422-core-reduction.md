# Conditional zero-private (4,2,2) reduction to a periodic Q core

Date: 2026-10-13. Status: independently reviewed VALID under the stated source hypotheses; [component review](../reviews/contact-right-angle-zero-private-generic-hostile-review.md). No unrestricted zero-private classification claimed.

Assume an edge-to-edge plane tiling with P lengths (x,u,v,y,w) all five pairwise distinct, every P type R (Q on u,v,w), and every Q having exactly two QQ sides. Let Q's native alphabet be {u,v,w}, with one value H occurring four times and the other two values occurring twice each. Then there exists an admitted tiling of the same shapes with a periodic Q scaffold and uniformly bounded anchored complementary strips. Applying the independently reviewed anchored-strip completion gives a periodic escape. This is an existence statement about a possibly different tiling.

## A clean actual-contact limit

In every translated square, degree-two QQ counting and the angle ledger give p=2q+O(R), with uniform boundary errors. Each P has precisely one public side of each length u,v,w. For either low-multiplicity Q value L, its QQ native incidences therefore satisfy e_L=2q-p+O(R)=O(R). This is a count of native incidences, not undirected edges; nonnegativity suffices. Bad QQ edges (of either low value) thus have a uniform linear bound. Separated-grid disks give arbitrarily large disks free of bad edges.

For fixed edge-to-edge polygons the native vertices have a uniform positive separation. Indeed choose rho less than the minimum, over their finitely many corners, of both incident side lengths and distance to every nonincident edge. Within the rho disk, every incident tile is exactly its angular corner wedge. The full star covers that disk by those wedges. A second native vertex cannot lie in a wedge interior, and cannot lie on a wedge ray because that ray is a native edge segment of length exceeding rho and the field is edge-to-edge. Consequently distinct native vertices are at least rho apart.

Translate the clean disks and take an indexed fixed-polygon placement limit. Uniform vertex separation prevents star mergers. Full native side contacts and their indexed endpoint stars persist: each finite limiting star has exactly the corresponding incident corner positions in late approximants, and there is no intervening native vertex along its whole edges. Thus the limit remains edge-to-edge, all-R, Q-degree-two, and has no QQ low-value contact. This is the needed actual-contact persistence; it is not inferred from compactness alone. In this cleaner field every Q's two QQ slots have length H. Its six public slots contain exactly two u, two v, two w.

## Tagged eligible masks and geometric symmetry

The local bevel ledger in `contact-right-angle-no-qq-zero-private-321-obstruction.md` applies without privacy. Distance-three gives the word (H,a,a,H,b,w,w,b), where {a,b}={u,v}. Distance-four gives (H,a,w,b,H,c,w,d), where b is opposite a and d is opposite c. Exact vertical/horizontal closure in the latter forces c=a (the alternative forces u=v), giving (H,a,w,b,H,a,w,b).

For the distance-three word, the only eligible H/H QQ mask is {0,3}, even if H equals a,b,or w. This follows by testing all cyclic starts against the same local tag patterns: additional a occurrences are consecutive in the short gap, additional b occurrences flank the long gap, and additional w occurrences are its consecutive middle slots. None supplies another valid H/H separated mask. The reflection of Q with edge-index action i -> 3-i exchanges its two QQ sides; its length word is invariant under that action. Ordered 45-degree directions and closure make this an actual Euclidean reflection, not an imposed combinatorial equivalence.

For the distance-four repeated word, Q is centrally symmetric. If H is u or v, the only eligible H/H mask is {0,4}. If H=w, the word is (w,a,w,b,w,a,w,b), and the eligible masks are exactly {0,4} and {2,6}. The reflection with edge-index action i -> 2-i exchanges those masks and is an actual Q isometry. Hence in every case the eligible H-side segments comprise a single orbit under actual polygon symmetries.

Any full QQ contact is therefore, as an actual placed-polygon geometry, represented by either reflection across its seam line or half-turn about its seam midpoint. The reflected mate gives equal outgoing public lengths at each seam endpoint, contradicting the unequal acute legs u,v of the P90 guest. The actual QQ mate is uniquely the half-turn. This conclusion permits native-mask ambiguity when H=w; it uses a symmetry orbit of actual geometric attachments instead of claiming one fixed indexed mask.

## Periodic scaffold and completion

The already reviewed Q-core proof needs: degree two, the allowed local masks, unique actual half-turn mates, and equality of all resulting chain period lengths. It does not use numeric privacy after these facts are established. Distance-three has one mask, so its chain period is fixed. Distance-four opposite masks have the same period length by the actual reflection above. Each chain is generated by successive seam-midpoint half-turns, giving a fixed nonzero translation every two steps. The existing proper-chain barrier argument excludes intersecting nonparallel chains. Their periods are therefore parallel and equal in length, so one common nonzero translation preserves the entire Q union. This asserts neither common indexed mask nor periodicity of the original filling.

The complement-band argument then uses only the actual edge-to-edge R filling: every P is whole-side anchored to Q, hence no outer half-plane is possible and all open complementary components are invariant strips of uniformly bounded width. A fundamental chain slice has finitely many whole-edge P anchors, with a uniform bound. The independently reviewed anchored-strip finite-state completion supplies a possibly new filling periodic along the common Q translation, with a uniform finite period bound across strips; choosing a common multiple gives an admitted periodic whole-plane tiling.
