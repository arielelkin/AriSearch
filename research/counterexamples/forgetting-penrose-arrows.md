# Counterexample to forgetting decorated matching rules

**Claim refuted:** If a strongly aperiodic decorated tiling partitions into
two unmarked convex supports, forgetting the decorations makes those two
supports an aperiodic protoset.

## Exact example

Take the standard arrow-decorated Penrose rhombs.  With unit side length,
their unmarked supports can be represented exactly in the real cyclotomic
field generated by \(\cos(\pi/5)\) and \(\sin(\pi/5)\):

\[
R_{36}=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),
(1+\cos36^\circ,\sin36^\circ),(\cos36^\circ,\sin36^\circ)\},
\]

\[
R_{72}=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),
(1+\cos72^\circ,\sin72^\circ),(\cos72^\circ,\sin72^\circ)\}.
\]

The standard arrow rules admit Penrose tilings and force nonperiodicity.  On
forgetting the arrows, however, either rhomb alone tiles the plane by the
lattice generated by its two side vectors.  That monohedral lattice tiling is
also a tiling admitted by \(\{R_{36},R_{72}\}\), so the unmarked pair is not
aperiodic.

## Verification and scope

For either \(R_\theta\), translates by
\((1,0)\) and \((\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\) cover the plane with disjoint
interiors.  This directly supplies the periodic witness; no finite search is
involved.

The example shows why a decorated-source recomposition must prove the
periodic-lift obligation in [research/lemmas/convex-cluster-fusion.md](../lemmas/convex-cluster-fusion.md).
Hidden arrows on seams do not constrain an arbitrary unmarked macrotiling.
