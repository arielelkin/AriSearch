It does not classify unrestricted partial contacts or prove that a unique local ring forces a global arrangement. **Verdict: valid local star and full-edge obstruction; global status unresolved.** P's successive directions `0,45,165,210,300` degrees give interior angles `(120,135,60,135,90)`. The stated positive exact side lengths close in `Q(sqrt(2),sqrt(3))`; Q's six directions and `(5,1,4,3,3,2)` close. The three sectors at the origin are 120 degrees each, with full radial contacts of lengths 5, 1, and 4. The exact isometries place the interiors in disjoint sectors. Given a trivalent full-edge star and a Q across P's length-5 ray, the adjacent Q side at the origin is 1 or 2. The side-4 completion needs a `(1,4)` corner; no tile has a `(2,4)` corner. The length-1 continuation follows under precisely those hypotheses.

The fourth-Q audit originally omitted reflections by requiring positive polygon orientation. The corrected artifact checks them. I independently reflected Q across each native length-3 side before attaching it to either exposed Qb side. For each of the four `(host side,new side)` choices, the reflected placement has no interior intersection with P, Qa, or Qb. Together with the four orientation-preserving placements there are **eight** legal full-side fourth-Q branches. This is a materially broader local branch set than the first script reported.

The later full-edge obstruction checks out. P's side 2 has length approximately 8.094 and is its only side of that length; all Q sides are at most 5. A full-edge mate must therefore be another P side 2, even if a copy is reflected. An endpoint pairing of 60 with 135 leaves 165 degrees, which cannot be filled by the available full corners of 60, 90, 120, and 135 degrees. The 135 endpoints must meet. Their remaining angle is 90, and only P has a 90-degree corner. Both rays beside the two incident 135 corners have length `l3`, while the sole P 90-degree corner has adjacent lengths `l3` and 4. This prevents a full-edge vertex. Consequently **no edge-to-edge plane tiling can contain P**. Since Q alone has no monohedral tiling as below, the pair has no edge-to-edge plane tiling. It remains possible for an unrestricted tiling to use partial contacts; the argument does not address those.

For P-only omission, the exact angle word passes only Rao types 2 and 5 among the fifteen reviewed table families, over cyclic and reflected relabelings. The type-2 cases require respectively `l0=l2` or `l1=l4`, and the type-5 case requires `l0=l4` and `l2=l1`; none holds. Thus P-only tilings are excluded **conditional on the imported complete Rao classification** and its reviewed Table 2 transcription, not by the local star search. Q-only omission can also be checked directly: every 120-degree corner in a tiling by Q must lie in a three-corner vertex, since a 120-degree corner plus a flat 180-degree contact leaves an unfillable 60-degree sector. Q's unordered adjacent-length pairs are `(2,5),(1,5),(1,4),(3,4),(3,3),(2,3)`. No length triple can contain the corner pair `(1,5)`: a third length would need both `(1,x)` and `(5,x)` in this list, and none exists. Every Q copy has that corner, ruling out Q-only plane tilings. This agrees with the cited monohedral hexagon specialization.

No mixed periodic carrier was audited. The edge-to-edge obstruction rules out a full-edge carrier, but a periodic carrier with partial contacts is still open. No plane extension of the P/Q/Q patch was proved. Repository word comparisons support the limited deduplication statements; they are not a literature novelty audit.
