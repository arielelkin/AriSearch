## Unique slots and actual QQ transport

Each private side length is unique within Q: another slot with that value would also be private, contradicting exactly two distinct private slots. Native reduction therefore makes every Q have exactly those two QQslots and whole-side contacts. The two opposite-side congruent placements across a unique side are line reflection or midpoint half-turn. At a reflected QQcontact, both outgoing QPside lengths at either endpoint are equal. They are whole contacts to the two acute Plegs at the P90sector, implyingu=v. Thus only half-turn placement survives. This is a geometry argument; native indices are identified by unique lengths and do not impose a mark.

Following an indexed nonbacktracking QQchain crosses i,j,i,jand two steps translate by2(mi-mj). Distinct side midpoints are distinct, so this period is nonzero. An even closed component would force a bounded polygon invariant under a nonzero translation. An odd closure would force central symmetry of Q, impossible because a unique-length side must then have an equal opposite side. Thus no finite component or exceptional congruence loop survives. Reflections of the initial placement merely rotate/reflect the same period vector and preserve its length.

## Proper cores, contacts and parallelism

Two Q tiles meeting only at a corner would have two135sectors. The unique compatible full star also has one P90sector, in which the Qsectors share a positive ray and hence an entire QQside. A Qcorner in another side interior is forbidden; two side interiors cannot have an isolated transverse crossing with disjoint tile interiors. Thus nonadjacent Qtiles cannot create unnoticed point-contact intersections of core paths.

Within each Q, the centroid-to-two-side-midpoint path is simple and lies in the interior except at seam midpoints. Convexity and positive side separation justify that claim. Joining these paths along a chain gives a proper embedded line because its finite two-tile motif repeats under a nonzero translation and nonneighbor tiles do not meet. The path is contained in a bounded-width slab and runs to opposite infinities parallel to its period. Width depends only on fixed Q.

The proper-line separation fact is applicable, not an assumed component classification: a proper embedded line lying in a horizontal slab and running to opposite horizontal infinities separates the two far horizontal halfplanes. One can close a sufficiently long central subarc outside a large rectangle and use the Jordan curve theorem; backtracking inside the slab does not change the separation. A second line asymptotic to a nonparallel direction has its two ends in the opposite far halfplanes and must intersect the first. Since different chain cores are disjoint, this proves parallelism. Their equal period lengths then give a common vector up to sign, and the union of all Qchains is periodic under that vector.
