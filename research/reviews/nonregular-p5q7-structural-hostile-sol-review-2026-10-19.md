## Independent checks

1. **Unrestricted contacts and stars.** Every angle is strictly obtuse and below 180 degrees, so the edge-forcing proof applies with reflections allowed. Direct enumeration of unordered triples from {100,110,115,125,130,135} gives exactly A,B,C,D,E as stated. Neither three P nor three Q sectors can sum to 360.
2. **Pointwise matching.** For a fixed P, PP edges have two distinct endpoint incidences. At each PPQ corner exactly one incident edge is PP; at PQQ corners neither is. Thus its number of PPQ corners is twice its PP degree. Both 115 corners are PPQ, both 100 corners are PQQ, and a C-type 110 would make the count three. Hence C is impossible and PP degree is exactly one. The two 115 corners must bound that edge. This argument uses neither cyclic-order assumptions nor orientation restrictions.
3. **Q alternatives.** With C removed, exactly k of the three Q130 corners are E. Every other Q corner has exactly one incident QQ side. Consequently 2 deg_QQ=7-k, and 0<=k<=3 gives precisely k=1 or k=3. Cyclic side-indicator consistency does not immediately eliminate k=3: the four non-E corners can occur in even-length runs between its three E corners. No pointwise exclusion has been smuggled in.
4. **Uniform boundary bounds and defects.** Tiles meeting a fixed-width boundary strip lie wholly in a boundedly wider strip; disjoint interiors and positive minimum area give O(R+1) tiles uniformly in window centre. Star counts inherit this bound since each tile has at most seven corners. The incidence equations give d=e=N_P+O(R), a=N_Q+O(R), a+2d=3N_Q+O(R); hence N_P-N_Q=O(R), all four star counts agree up to O(R), and e=N_Q+2Z+O(R) implies Z=O(R), uniformly. Boundary signs cannot exclude isolated defects. Here O(R) is for R>=1.
5. **Purification and compactness.** If every square of one fixed side L contained a defective Q marked point, a grid of disjoint such squares in a large window would force quadratic many defects. Boundary ambiguity can be avoided by assigning grid boundaries or doubling L. Thus arbitrarily large defect-free squares exist. Translating their centres and taking a subsequence gives a tiling by the same fixed metric shapes: bounded diameters and positive areas bound the number of tiles intersecting any bounded set, while tile poses lie in compact sets locally. A diagonal limit covers the plane and preserves disjoint interiors. A defective tile in the limit would have a finite neighbourhood of full-edge neighbours, contradicting defect freedom in the approximants; the obtuse edge-forcing lemma prevents a changed limiting contact type. Defect-free marked-point squares suffice after trimming their boundaries by the maximum tile diameter. The alternative finite-local-complexity explanation is also sound modulo global rigid motion, since each full-edge adjacency has finitely many side/isometry choices.
6. **Dimer geometry and torus arithmetic.** The union of matched convex P tiles shares precisely their common edge. It has six surviving convex corners and two 230-degree reflex corners; its exterior-turn sum is 2(80+80+70)-100=360 degrees. At each reflex corner E supplies exactly Q130. For a genuine rank-two translational quotient enlarged to avoid identifications, incidence equations give N_P=N_Q=N and a=b=d=e=N, so V=4N, E=6N, F=2N. The canonical matching requires even N. N=2 satisfies these displayed integer equations, without implying a realizable quotient. Finite rotations from turns divisible by five degrees provide no translational return.
