# Hostile review: eight-child radial topology gate

Date: 2026-10-20. Verdict: **valid after two explicit corrections to the report**. The graph obstruction and the two remaining 7P+H incidence orbits withstand independent audit. This is a finite convex-dissection preflight, not a geometric substitution or plane-tiling result.

## Corrections required

The native-side defect totals in the current search report are each one too small. For eight children, `7P+H` gives `sum(6-n_i)=7`, and `6P+2H` gives `6`. The defect identity is `sum(6-n_i)=1+B+2J+2D+H_flat`. With these corrected totals, `r=1,b=7` is impossible because `B+J>=7` makes the right side at least 8. The `6P+2H` inventory is impossible at `r=2,b=6` because the right side is at least 7. The `7P+H` inventory then reaches equality precisely at `B=6,J=D=H_flat=0`, as the report claims. With the uncorrected totals the displayed equality does not follow.

The interior-count split should state why `b<=1` cannot occur. If one child has all positive outer-boundary contact, every open parent-boundary interval belongs to it. Closure puts the entire parent boundary in that convex child, so the child equals the parent. That contradicts an eight-child positive-area partition. Thus `b>=2`.

## Independent topology check

The full exterior rim cycle is a valid **augmentation**, without assuming consecutive boundary children touch. Select one open parent-boundary contact arc for each boundary child and draw an exterior spoke from its child vertex to a common exterior vertex `O`. These spokes form an embedded star outside the parent, apart from short disjoint pieces within their respective boundary children. A thin regular neighborhood of the exterior star has one boundary circuit. Collapsing its leaf arcs to the child vertices gives a simple cycle through all `b>=3` boundary children while retaining every edge incident to an interior child. Existing boundary-boundary edges may be deleted and redrawn because the count uses only the new rim edges. At a boundary child, the exterior spoke and two rim edges can be attached in the exterior sector, separate from retained contacts drawn through the child interior. For `b=2`, one boundary-boundary edge suffices.

Each interior child has at least its native number of distinct positive-contact neighbors. A single convex neighbor cannot cover two different native sides, because the intersection of two convex children is itself convex with empty planar interior. For `r>=3`, the induced interior graph has at most `3r-6` edges. Therefore at least `5r-(3r-6)=2r+6` child edges touch an interior vertex. At `b>=3`, the spokes and full rim give at least `2r+6+2b=22` edges on nine vertices, exceeding 21. At `b=2`, the count is at least 21; if equal, the graph would be a nine-vertex triangulation containing the degree-two vertex `O`, impossible. This excludes `r>=3`.

For `r=2,b=6`, an interior hexagon has at least five boundary neighbors, and the other interior child at least four. Their boundary-neighbor sets intersect in at least three vertices; those three together with the two interior vertices and `O` contain `K_3,3`. Both interior children are therefore pentagons. If they did not share a positive side, both would have at least five boundary neighbors and the same contradiction follows. Once they share a side, each has at least four boundary neighbors. Three shared boundary neighbors would again give `K_3,3`, so the sets each have size four, their union is all six boundary children, and their intersection has size two.

At the corrected 7P+H equality, six simple open-side boundary seam nodes and eight trivalent internal seam nodes remain, with no straight child-angle incidence. Each boundary child has one outer arc. No interior child reaches the parent boundary even at a point: an isolated open-side touch needs at least two incident seam edges and would contribute to `D`; a parent-corner touch would contribute to `J`. Each native interior-pentagon side has exactly one whole-side neighbor contact: the five distinct neighbors exhaust its five sides, and a split side would create a straight incidence. Every consecutive boundary pair has a positive contact along the seam issuing from its intervening simple boundary node. Thus the six-edge rim is now an actual contact cycle.

The resulting nine-vertex contact-plus-`O` graph has `1+8+6+6=21` edges, hence is a triangulation. The two shared boundary children lie opposite on the six-cycle. One direct way to see this is that the four boundary neighbors of either inner vertex must be consecutive around the cycle in a disk triangulation; the other inner vertex uses the complementary four, and the two overlap at opposite endpoints. I also replayed the independent Sage enumeration in the paired script: it returns six labeled planar choices, all with opposite shared children.

For a boundary child with `k` parent corners on its sole outer arc, `k+1` native sides lie on the parent boundary. A shared child has four internal contacts and therefore native side count `k+5`; an exclusive child has three and count `k+4`. The five parent corners consequently force exactly two hexagon placements up to the dihedral symmetry of the incidence map: H shared with one parent corner, or H exclusive with two parent corners. The latter is a genuine topology option and must be tested in any metric gate.
