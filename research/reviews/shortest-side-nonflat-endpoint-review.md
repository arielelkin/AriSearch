# Independent review: shortest side with non-flat endpoints

Reviewed: [root lemma](../lemmas/shortest-side-nonflat-endpoint-obstruction.md).
Verdict: valid. It is a local whole-side forcing theorem, not a density claim.

Any native neighbour side having positive collinear overlap with the
designated side lies on the same support line. If it extended past either
designated endpoint, that endpoint would be strictly inside the neighbour
side and would see its flat180 sector. A positive native angle precludes
two flat sectors, so the native180 multiset criterion detects every such
possibility. Non-flat-eligibility of both endpoints therefore confines the
entire neighbour side between them. Weak minimum length then forces exact
segment equality. This argument includes multiple equal-length native
partner types; the root correctly requires all of them to be checked.

Fixed positive area/inscribed radius and bounded diameter justify local
finiteness. Strict native convexity rules out a bent or multiply segmented
straight boundary of one neighbour. No global edge-to-edge, orientation,
frequency or finite-local-complexity assumption is used.

For the current samples, unique minimum A1 has endpoints140.5 and147.5,
neither occurring in the sole native180 multiset. Reflection and half-turn
same-A1 joins fail at the stated79 and72 remainders. Thus no A occurs at
all; pure B is locally impossible by its complete angle-star census.
The root correctly limits the general parameter family to the minimum
side locus unless an additional argument is supplied. No correction needed.
