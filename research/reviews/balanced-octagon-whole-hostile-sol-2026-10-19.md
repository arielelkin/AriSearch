# Whole-theorem hostile Sol audit: balanced complementary-angle octagons

Date: 2026-10-19. **Verdict: PASS for the exact conditional theorem.**

Reviewed `research/lemmas/balanced-octagon-complementary-angle-periodicity-2026-10-19.md`, the complementary-angle necessary theorem, both analytic search reports, and their three independent review reports. I sought a counterexample arising from an unproved common return, a guest matching assumption, unequal metric side lengths, reflected root ambiguities, sparse defects, resonance, or omission. No substantive flaw was found. This is a whole-proof stitching audit, rather than a claim that any surviving shape actually tiles.

## Independent checks and logical dependencies

I independently regenerated balanced words from permutations of AAAABBCC, reduced by explicit rotation and reversal, and filtered by even non-A runs and at most two AA sides. There are 33 dihedral words and exactly the seven displayed survivors. No B/C exchange was used. An independent rational affine-angle enumeration of all corner triples gives PPA and PBC identically and BBB only at alpha=120. The angle bounds exclude both higher valence and a straight-side contact. Thus unrestricted contacts and reflections introduce no additional vertex type.

At resonance, balanced incidence forces BBB to have at most boundary-order count. For a fixed word admitting a tiling this suffices for the necessary word gate: a word whose every native local assignment uses a BBB corner would force quadratic count. A zero-BBB local assignment requires even non-A runs. After the seven-word gate, the stronger pointwise exclusion follows because every non-A run has length two and every B has an incident edge toward A. Such an edge faces P and cannot border BBB. There is no circular use of pointwise absence to obtain the word list.

The incidence identity p=2q+O(R+1) and two AA sides per Q give a nonnegative capacity deficit of boundary order in every translated square. Enlarging by a fixed tile diameter handles discrepancies between tile anchors and incidences. For each fixed L, the union of L-neighborhoods of defects has only boundary-order area in a large square, leaving a clean center. Fixed positive tile area and bounded diameter supply a locally finite compactness subsequence. Full-side AA receipts survive by extracting the finite native incidence choices. The resulting plane tiling is saturated. This argument neither assumes a frequency limit nor declares the source tiling saturated.

In the saturated tiling each P has exactly two PP sides and one selected AA-facing side. The physical endpoint rule across PP edges gives alternating turns for disjoint-AA words and ++-- for AAA words. In the latter case the two AA anchors of each Q form one marked PP edge, and the marked edges form a perfect matching. Nonzero two-step or four-step displacement excludes finite components. Every path step has positive projection on its return axis. Distinct paths lie in disjoint tile interiors apart from their own PP midpoints. A proper graph within bounded distance of its axis separates the plane; a path with nonparallel axis has opposite tails on opposite sides and must intersect it. Hence all axes are parallel. Fixed step geometry fixes the return length, so every component returns under the same vector up to sign. Both signs are symmetries, giving one common vector without requiring aligned phases.

For AABBAACC, the two AA directions differ by 432-2alpha degrees, strictly between180 and216. They cannot both be parallel to the common backbone axes. This rules out the purified tiling, and therefore the assumed original tiling.

For AABCAABC, opposite unit PQ vectors cancel and the two QQ vectors are antiparallel. Closure forces equal QQ lengths and actual central symmetry. The forward B/C anchor label therefore specifies a rooted metric placement even allowing both native AA roots and reflections. The PBC equation gives period two in P steps.

For AABCAACB, transverse closure is (x-y)sin(216-alpha)=0, with sine nonzero throughout the interval. Equal QQ lengths give actual reflection symmetry. The B,B and C,C anchor types each determine one rooted placement, and PBC flips type after two P steps, returning after four.

For AAABCACB and AAACBABC, I checked that the relevant closure component is axial, with respective nonzero coefficients cos(144-alpha) and cos(alpha-108). The correction recorded in the earlier review is present in the source report. Equal QQ lengths give actual metric reflection symmetry, so the two-AA root determines a unique placement and the four-step P return fixes all Q attachments. There is no assumption that an angle-word symmetry alone implies metric symmetry.

For AAABBACC and AAABCABC, the physical pentagon side sequence is PQ,PP,PQ,PQ,PP with selected side0. Choose the marked edge as side1 of the even pentagon. Its other endpoint has the isolated-A guest, which faces side2 and reaches that pentagon's PBC corner. On its mate the same guest faces side3 and reaches the other PBC corner. The remaining PQ sides belong to the preceding and following dimer owners. This proves the two PBC corners see the same guest, without identifying that guest with a neighboring owner or prescribing any transverse matching. Each owner's external AAA labels are complementary, and each guest's isolated-A labels are complementary. The two PBC equations consequently force s_(k+1)=s_(k-1). The unique AAA root has at most two reflected metric placements, distinguished by the forward B/C angle since B differs from C throughout the open interval. Thus recurrence of the label really gives recurrence of the whole physical tile after two dimers. Additional QQ metric equations only restrict realizability; they cannot introduce a third rooted placement or weaken the recurrence. No shift-equivariance, bounded displacement, or same-carrier property of the guest matching is assumed.

Finally, every Q owns its AA anchor or marked dimer. Its proven return is therefore accounted for on an actual P component. Taking the common vector, or twice that vector for AABCAACB, maps every P and Q to another tile, with inverse return as well. There is no unanchored Q subsystem whose period remains unproved.

## Certified scope

For every fixed strictly convex positive metric octagon Q with the stated inventory, every real108<alpha<126 (including120), and a unit regular pentagon P, allowing arbitrary isometries, reflections, unrestricted initial contacts, and omission of either species: if the pair tiles the plane, it has a plane tiling with a nonzero translation. AABBAACC admits no plane tiling. The other conditional periods in the theorem are certified for a purified limit tiling: two P steps for AABCAABC; four for AABCAACB and all four AAA words.

Omission creates no exception. P-only tiling has no allowed vertex star; Q-only tiling is impossible for this inventory because its A corners require PPA. More generally the incidence and coverage estimates force quadratic Q count rather than a presumed positive limiting frequency. No uniform lower bound on metric side lengths across different choices of Q is needed: all compactness and boundary constants concern one fixed positive metric Q at a time.

The proof establishes existence of a translationally periodic tiling conditional on tileability. It does not establish two independent periods, periodicity of every source tiling, tileability of any listed metric realization, a classification outside this inventory, or an unrestricted two-convex-polygon impossibility theorem. No counterexample or hidden global assumption was found within the certified scope.
