# Hostile review: universal eight-child pentagon partition

Date: 2026-10-20. Verdict: **VALID**, subject to the already reviewed convex-partition defect identity, forest gate, and planar exterior-rim augmentation. This reviews [the universal lemma](../lemmas/eight-child-pentagon-one-hexagon-2026-10-20.md), including arbitrary partial contacts and isolated parent-boundary touches. It asserts no geometric realization or plane tiling.

The proof does not silently assume that the children are pentagons or hexagons. Initially it uses only `n_i >= 5` and at least one `n_i >= 6`, giving `L = sum(6-n_i) <= 7`. The forest gate gives `r >= 1`; convexity of a sole boundary-contact child gives `b >= 2`. If `r = 1`, seven boundary labels imply `B+J >= 7`, so the exact defect identity gives `L >= 8`, impossible. If `r >= 3`, the planar contact graph has at least `2r+6` edges incident to interior children. Adding `O`, its `b` spokes, and the exterior rim yields at least 22 edges on nine vertices when `b >= 3`. For `b = 2`, it yields at least 21 edges, and equality is impossible because `O` has degree two in a maximal planar graph. This covers every possible `r`, including `r = 6`.

Consequently `r = 2`, `b = 6`, and six boundary labels give `L >= 7`. Equality throughout forces seven pentagons and exactly one hexagon, with `B=6` and `J=D=H_flat=0`. In particular, a single heptagon gives `L <= 6`, and two hexagons also give `L <= 6`; neither can exploit a partial contact or isolated boundary touch to evade this count. Those features add nonnegative defect terms and cannot reduce the six required boundary transitions.

The remaining contact deductions are valid. Each interior native side has a positive-contact neighbor, and convex intersection makes neighbors on distinct sides distinct. An interior hexagon would have at least five boundary neighbors; the other interior child would have at least four, producing three common boundary neighbors and a `K_{3,3}` with `O`. The two interior pentagons must contact each other by the same argument. Each then has exactly four boundary neighbors, with exactly two common ones. A split interior side would create a straight child-angle incidence, ruled out by `H_flat=0`, so their mutual contact and all eight interior-to-boundary contacts are along whole native sides.

The six boundary labels have exactly six transitions, hence one open boundary arc per label. Each transition is a simple open-side seam endpoint. Its inward seam separates the adjacent boundary children, so the rim cycle is now made of actual positive contacts. The resulting contact graph with `O` has exactly 21 edges and is a disk triangulation; the four boundary neighbors of each interior pentagon are consecutive, and the two common boundary children are opposite. This was also checked against the [independent finite graph enumeration](../searches/eight-child-radial-topology-2026-10-20.py). An interior child cannot touch the parent at an isolated point: an open-side touch would give boundary seam degree at least two and `D>0`, while a corner touch would give `J>0`.

For a boundary child whose sole arc crosses `k` parent corners, `J=H_flat=0` makes its outer side contribution exactly `k+1`. The shared boundary children have four additional native sides and the exclusive children three. Hence their native counts are respectively `k+5` and `k+4`. The unique hexagon is either shared with `k=1` or exclusive with `k=2`; all other corner slots are forced. These are the two stated dihedral orbits. The nine-child corollary follows for a child with at least seven sides from this eight-child result, the reviewed seven-child and six-child theorems, and the forest gate's six-child minimum under the same hypotheses.

No unresolved objection found. The conclusion is topological and says nothing about whether either eight-child incidence map can be embedded with specified tile shapes.
