Since `pi/3<theta=atan(2)<3pi/8`, every native corner exceeds `theta`, while `pi-g=pi/4`, `pi-a=theta`, and `pi-d=pi-2theta` do not reach the smallest native corner. The required lower bound `theta>pi/3` is explicitly incorporated in the lemma's opening angle paragraph.

For one interior breakpoint, both opposite native sides overhang the respective B endpoints because each has length at least `sqrt(2)` and each BB subinterval is shorter. At an overhung B endpoint, flat+b requires c; its arm along the host BA edge must be CD of length `sqrt(5)`, since CG would end at G strictly inside the host BA edge and flat+g leaves only `pi/4`. Its other arm CG of length `sqrt(2)` forms the guard beyond the B endpoint. A guest far A, D or G must coincide with that guard's G: either order of strict separation creates a forbidden flat+a, flat+d or flat+g. For `rr`, the two guest RA arms demand `4=3sqrt(2)`. For `bc`, a horizontal CG arm places G strictly inside a guard; with horizontal CD, a horizontal guest BB arm creates a G interior to the guest BA arm. The remaining two horizontal length-`sqrt(5)` arms demand `2sqrt(5)=3sqrt(2)`. Both equalities are false. Cyclic reversal merely exchanges the left/right assignment and cannot change these equations.

There cannot be two or more interior contact transitions along a BB side in a locally finite tiling. Consecutive transitions delimit one opposite tile's complete native side inside a strict subinterval of BB. Its length would be less than `sqrt(2)`. Local finiteness follows from the fixed two convex shapes: each contains a disk of a fixed positive radius, and disks from tiles meeting a bounded region lie in another bounded region and have disjoint interiors. Thus the exactly-one theorem plus this shortest-edge fact leaves a single guest native side covering BB. **This does not imply that side has equal length or that other contacts are edge-to-edge.**

That P's arm along the remaining host BA segment has length at least `sqrt(2)`, while the remaining segment has length `sqrt(5)-sqrt(2)<sqrt(2)`.
