# Independent hostile review: bipartite two-node Ammann pentagons

Date: 2026-10-14. Reviewer: terra_interfaces, independently of the scheme
authors/root. Verdict: VALID on the stated construction and obstruction scope.

The source argument is conditional on the already reviewed infinite legal
marked unit thin/thick rhomb covering, which was not reproved here. Given
that source, the construction yields a globally compatible two-class strict
convex pentagon partition. Both output classes individually have explicit
periodic full-plane tilings. Therefore the construction does not solve the
unmarked aperiodic-pair problem, and no translation-equivariant intrinsic
decoder to the legal marked source can work for every admitted output tiling.

The independent checker uses SymPy, not the producer's Sage implementation,
and performs exact support-line tests against every nonincident vertex. It
checks all source sectors are strictly convex/simple, inside the source
rhomb, pairwise interior-disjoint by separating axes, and their areas sum
to the source area. This establishes a genuine partition rather than merely
a turn/area coincidence. It then checks the triangle and receiving quad
are interior-disjoint, their glued five-vertex polygon passes all strict
support tests, and areas add exactly. All calculations pass in both thin
and thick geometries. No floating-point sign judgments are used.

The checker verifies the physical unit metric despite the scaled y
coordinate, and reconstructs the triangle apex from the unit edge normal.
In particular both base endpoints are actual strict pentagon corners; the
far endpoint obstruction from the earlier mixed-contact cycles is avoided,
not assumed away. Reflection through the rhomb diagonal interchanges its
two unit edge directions; it swaps the source edge roles without changing
the output congruence class. A halfturn pairs the two triangles and two
quads inside each source rhomb. These symmetries exhaust the alternate
coloring, orientation, and allowed flip choices here. Different source
angles give distinct output areas, hence two different congruence classes.

Global compatibility follows from a bipartite source vertex graph. A finite
simple edge cycle bounds finitely many source quadrilateral faces; counting
face-edge incidences makes its length even. Connectedness and local
finiteness hold for the unit edge-to-edge source. Choosing either vertex
coloring makes each source edge a triangle edge on one side and a quad
edge on the other, since the boundary directions reverse across the edge.
The common physical triangle is congruent independently of the neighboring
source type. Exactly one triangle attaches to each quad and none is used
twice. Thus no missing vertex compatibility condition is hidden in the
edgewise argument: both rhombs were completely partitioned before gluing.

For each single source angle, the lattice generated by the two unit rhomb
edges carries an exact rhomb tiling and a parity coloring of its integer
vertex coordinates. The same surgery is globally valid there, and two
times either lattice generator preserves colors and output polygons. These
are two independent nonzero periods, with every output belonging to only
one pentagon class. No Ammann arrows are required on that carrier; the
outputs in the problem are unmarked. Consequently increasing source patch
depth or changing the positive altitude within the convex range cannot
repair the scheme's omission failure.

The recognition conclusion is correctly scoped. A universal
translation-equivariant decoder would preserve the carrier's translations
and create a periodic legal marked source, contradicting the reviewed
source forcing theorem. A coloring or remembered subdivision is auxiliary
data and does not supply an intrinsic decoder. This review does not
classify every decoder concept with arbitrary global symmetry breaking,
or every asymmetric/type-dependent future surgery.
