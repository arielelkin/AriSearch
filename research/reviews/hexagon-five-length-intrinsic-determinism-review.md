# Fresh hostile review: intrinsic geometric determinism

Reviewer: `primary_literature`, 2026-10-09. The reviewer did not develop or
modify the proof. Reviewed
[the conditional lemma](../lemmas/hexagon-five-length-intrinsic-determinism.md)
as saved on this date, independently of the former absolute-orientation
finite-state lemma.

## Exact claim and hypothesis ledger

The claim is conditional. An edge-to-edge plane tiling by finitely many fixed
compact convex polygon classes has a connected full-side tile graph. Every
tile has one of finitely many intrinsic indexed reference states and a specified
placement isometry. For each reachable state and native side slot, the state,
slot and **complete** relative placement isometry of the full-side neighbor
are fixed. This includes both endpoint images, the relative orthogonal map,
displacement, and neighbor indexing. Under those hypotheses the tiling has
two independent translational periods. Absolute orientations need not be
finite. Polygon self-symmetries must be resolved in the stated indexing data.

The proof does not establish its neighbor-map hypotheses for an arbitrary
polygon pair. Finite symbolic states, finite allowed neighbor sets, or
uniqueness of the neighbor's shape type alone do not supply those hypotheses.

## Hostile checks and findings

**Propagation.** For equal-state tiles U,W, the proposed isometry
I=g_W g_U^{-1} carries every indexed side of U to the corresponding indexed
side of W. The same complete relative map h_(s,i) gives
I g_V=g_W h_(s,i)=g_X for their neighbors. This calculation remains valid
when any of g_U,g_W,h is orientation reversing. No unrecorded orientation
parity state is needed. Iterating through the connected full-side graph
carries every source tile, with its state and frame, to a tile in the same
tiling. Since the source collection covers the whole plane and I is a plane
isometry, its images cover the plane. A tile omitted from the image collection
would leave its interior uncovered, so the resulting map preserves the whole
framed tiling rather than just a forward subcollection.

**Path consistency.** The proof does not assume freely chosen symbolic
transition maps are globally realizable. The original actual tiling supplies
the neighbors along every traversed path. Thus the propagation calculation
never needs an extra holonomy assertion for arbitrary transition words.

**Connectedness in geometric applications.** For an edge-to-edge plane tiling
by finitely many fixed compact polygons, local finiteness follows from a
positive minimum area and a bounded maximum diameter. A compact path joining
interior points of two tiles can be perturbed to avoid the finitely many
vertices it encounters and to cross boundary edges transversely. Each crossing
then enters a full-side neighbor. This proves the full-side tile graph is
connected. Vertex-only adjacency is not being substituted for it. The
conditional lemma explicitly assumes connectedness, so it does not rely on
this consequence for arbitrary subdivided contacts.

**Discrete symmetry group.** If a sequence of tiling symmetries tends to the
identity, an interior point of a fixed tile eventually belongs to the interior
of each image of that tile. Interior-disjointness forces those images to be
the same tile. A full-area compact polygon has only finitely many self
isometries, so convergence to the identity then forces eventual identity.
This excludes the proposed failure mode of a nondiscrete group generated by
infinitely many orientations. Equal-state propagation gives finitely many
tile orbits; a finite union of compact orbit representatives has translates
under this group covering the plane. Cocompactness is therefore obtained
from the actual tiling, not assumed from the intrinsic alphabet.

**Translations.** Let N be the translation kernel of the linear-part
homomorphism. If N were trivial, its orientation-preserving subgroup would
inject into the abelian SO(2). Any nonidentity member is a rotation. Every
commuting rotation fixes its unique center, making the subgroup discrete in
a compact circle group, hence finite. The entire group has index at most
two over that subgroup and would also be finite, contrary to cocompactness.
Thus N is nontrivial. If N had rank one, every orthogonal part would send a
primitive generator to itself or its negative. Only four orthogonal maps
have this property, so there are at most four translation cosets. Images of
a compact fundamental collection under those cosets remain bounded in the
perpendicular coordinate; they cannot cover the plane. N has rank two.
Reflections and glide reflections introduce no exception to either argument.

## Defects, counterexamples, repairs, and uncertainty

No counterexample or proof defect was found under the complete stated
hypotheses. No repair was made by this reviewer. Removing complete geometric
neighbor uniqueness, removing finite intrinsic states, or applying the lemma
to a disconnected partial-contact graph would leave a substantive missing
step; none is licensed by this proof. The lemma supplies no classification
of which convex shapes satisfy its hypotheses. No priority or novelty claim
is audited or asserted here.

**Verdict: VALID, conditional exactly as stated.** Its concrete application
is separately reviewed in
[the private-repeat review](hexagon-five-length-private-repeat-periodicity-review.md).
