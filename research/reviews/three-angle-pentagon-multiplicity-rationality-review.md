# Fresh hostile review: unequal three-angle pentagon multiplicities

**Verdict: valid.** Reviewed on 2026-10-09 by the independent hexagon-structure
agent, which did not develop the claim.

## Exact claim audited

[Root lemma](../lemmas/three-angle-pentagon-multiplicity-rationality.md): if two
convex pentagons together have exactly three interior-angle values and their
multiplicity rows differ, existence of any admitted unrestricted plane tiling
forces all three angle values to be rational multiples of pi. The tiling may
omit a shape and may have partial-side contacts and reflections.

## Attempts to invalidate

The two shape rows are independent over Q: proportionality and common row
sum 5 would imply equality. Both shape angle-sum equations remain available
when only one tile type occurs. At a native vertex, local finiteness follows
from the positive minimum tile area and bounded diameter. Any incident copy
contributes a corner sector or a half-plane sector; there is at most one
half-plane sector beside the selected genuine positive corner. Thus the
integer corner row has normalized angle sum h=1 or 2. No edge-to-edge or
frequency assumption occurs.

If that corner row were in the rational span of the two shape rows, its
coordinate sum would be 5h/3. This fails integrality for both admissible h.
Therefore the three integer rows have rank 3, and their rational right-hand
sides determine rational normalized angles. The proof does not impose a
valence bound; very small angles and arbitrarily many sectors do not alter
the integrality contradiction. Selecting any present native corner avoids
rare-type or omitted-type loopholes.

I checked the equal-row and four-angle boundaries: the rank argument gives
no rationality claim there. Rationality itself is never converted into
periodicity. The formal equal-row angle list is explicitly not asserted to
be a closed polygon or a global tiling.

## Defects and remaining uncertainty

No defect or repair found. No computation or external classification theorem
is required. The theorem is only a necessary angle reduction and leaves the
main unrestricted aperiodic-pair problem open.
