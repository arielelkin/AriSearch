## Closure and equality cases

Using u0=(1,0),u1=(0,1),u2=(−1,1),u3=(−1,0),u4=(0,−1),u5=(1,−1) in a positively oriented triangular basis gives closure coefficients

    s0−s2−s3+s5 = 0,
    s1+s2−s4−s5 = 0.

These imply exactly s0−s3=s2−s5=s4−s1. For two distinct lengths a<b, a nonzero common difference is ±(b−a), forcing every one of the three opposite pairs in the same indicated order. Thus the unique noncentral words are alternating a,b (and their shifts). A one-length member is central. There is no omitted resonant ratio between the two lengths.

For zero common difference, the edges are u,v,w,−u,−v,−w. The six listed vertices satisfy v_i+v_(i+3)=u+v+w, proving actual central symmetry, not merely opposite edge parallelism. The converse is immediate from central symmetry and the unique opposite heading.

## Unrestricted contacts and phase/reflection attack

Local finiteness holds because fixed positive-area compact shapes have uniform positive inscribed-disk radius and bounded diameter. At a corner in another side's relative interior, the 180° complementary half-plane cannot be partitioned into 120° corners and/or straight 180° sectors once one genuine corner is present. Partial overlaps of sides would have such an endpoint. Hence full-edge trivalence follows under the actual unrestricted definition.

The length graph uses actual numerical equalities, so it does not assume the palettes coincide, are disjoint, or have rationally independent values. At most two nonloop edges cannot form a triangle and are bipartite, including duplicated edges. At each trivalent vertex, the three ray lengths require all three consecutive pairs to be edges of this graph. If any ray lengths coincide, that would require a loop, which is absent. Otherwise they form an ordinary triangle. Both are impossible. Arbitrary rotations change headings and reflections reverse ordering, but neither changes this unordered corner condition. Omission of either species cannot avoid it when both are noncentral: using one graph edge remains bipartite.

## Periodic escape: topology and metric audit

The central hexagon vertices are exactly

    0,u,u+v,u+v+w,v+w,w.

Pair edges k and k+3 in reversed boundary orientation for k=0,1,2. The endpoint translations are respectively v+w,w−u,−(u+v). Their group is generated by U=u+v,V=v+w because w−u=V−U. The vertex classes are {0,2,4} and {1,3,5}, each with exactly three 120° sectors. Edge interiors are nonsingular, and the reversed boundary gluing is orientable. One face, three edges, and two vertices give Euler characteristic zero; equivalently these pairings give a flat torus directly.

For |u|=x,|v|=y,|w|=z>0 at headings 0°,60°,120°,

    det(U,V)=sqrt(3)(xy+xz+yz)/2>0.

The developed universal cover of this compact flat torus is the complete simply connected flat plane. Its lifted face decomposition supplies an actual covering tiling with disjoint interiors, and deck translations include the independent U,V. Thus the proof establishes whole-plane coverage; it does not infer it from matching edges alone. The one central member may be used alone, as permitted by the problem.
