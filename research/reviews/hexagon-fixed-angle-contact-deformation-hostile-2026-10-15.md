# Independent hostile review: fixed-angle contact deformation

Date: 2026-10-15. Reviewer: fresh independent agent, not the author of the deformation lemma or its A6/B5 dependencies.

**Verdict: VALID.** This is a conditional periodic-escape theorem for a specified source tiling and separating kernel vector. It is not a classification of arbitrary repeated-side hexagon pairs or a resolution of the main problem.

## Exact claim reviewed

The claim is [fixed-angle deformation transfers a periodic hexagon escape](../lemmas/hexagon-fixed-angle-contact-deformation-2026-10-15.md). Let A,B be distinct genuine convex hexagons, each with at least five distinct native side lengths, and let T be an everywhere-trivalent full-native-edge plane tiling. Fix reference indices on its occurrences. Form the equivalence relation E on the twelve native side slots generated by all seams occurring anywhere in T. Each equivalence class has one original numerical length. Let M send a class-length variation to the two planar native vector-closure sums. If a vector in ker M separates every repeated pair of native A side lengths, then the original unmarked pair has a doubly periodic full-side plane tiling. The symmetric statement holds for B.

I read the governing problem and protocol, the current checkpoint, the complete deformation lemma, the A6/B5 synthesis, its three repeat-separation component proofs, the earlier A6/B6 torus proof, and the intrinsic-determinism and defect-free-limit dependencies. The new lemma introduces no proof-relevant finite enumeration. Existing finite classifications are imported in their recorded independently reviewed scope; this review does not claim a new replay of those classifications.

## Audited proof

**The equivalence classes and the perturbation.** Every generator of E joins equal original full-side lengths. Transitivity therefore gives one well-defined positive original length x_C for each class. Native closure is exactly Mx=0. Hence x+epsilon v closes both polygon boundaries whenever Mv=0. For sufficiently small epsilon all lengths stay positive, and the finitely many strict native support inequalities remain strict. Thus the closed boundary still bounds a genuine convex hexagon with its indexed edge directions unchanged. In particular every native interior angle is unchanged exactly, not approximately.

There are finitely many pairs of native side slots. Originally unequal lengths remain unequal on a sufficiently small interval about zero. Originally equal A lengths with unequal variation coordinates separate for every nonzero epsilon. One can consequently select one nonzero epsilon simultaneously satisfying convexity, positivity, separation, and preservation of all original inequalities of lengths. The latter requirement is stronger than merely retaining the original seams, and is essential for transferring a different periodic witness back.

**Plane remetricization and completeness.** Use the original abstract polygonal cell complex of T, with exactly its original side identifications and corner incidences. Corresponding sides receive identical new lengths because each original seam belongs to E. Replacing each face with its new polygon therefore defines a length metric on the same topological plane. Edge interiors are nonsingular, and every vertex remains nonsingular because its same three indexed angles still sum to 2pi.

The proof does not infer completeness just from local flatness. Choose an interior point of each native polygon, for example its vertex average, and triangulate to its six vertices. It remains interior under the small perturbation. The two original-to-new templates give finitely many nonsingular affine triangle maps. Their operator norms and inverse operator norms have one finite uniform bound. On a face side the map is the endpoint-preserving linear map along that side, independent of the adjacent template and of reflected occurrence placements. Thus the maps glue. The map and inverse distort lengths of polygonal paths by at most this common bound; taking intrinsic infima gives a global bilipschitz homeomorphism. Local finiteness follows already from fixed positive tile areas and bounded tile diameters, so compact paths can be handled with finite subdivisions or standard length approximation. The remetricized surface is complete because the original plane is complete. Being also simply connected and flat, it develops isometrically onto the Euclidean plane. This proves a new full-side tiling, rather than just arbitrary finite perturbed patches.

**Indexing and symmetries.** The construction needs a fixed native indexing for each source occurrence, not a canonical one. Facewise triangulations, seam classes, and closure equations use those fixed indices consistently. Reflections preserve the prescribed corner angles and are accommodated by composing the affine template with the occurrence isometry. No absolute orientation finiteness is asserted. In the stated at-least-five-distinct-length domain each reference hexagon in fact has no nonidentity polygon symmetry: any symmetry must preserve its at least four uniquely occurring side lengths, hence fix those distinct side slots, whereas a nonidentity dihedral action on a six-cycle cannot do so. The more general explicit indexing convention is nevertheless harmless.

**Distinct shape classes.** Noncongruence of A and B is open for this finite perturbation. One direct verification avoids a compactness shortcut: any congruence of genuine hexagons induces one of finitely many cyclic or reversed vertex correspondences. For each correspondence, equality of all vertex-distance data is a closed condition. If arbitrarily small perturbations were congruent, a subsequence would use the same correspondence and imply original congruence by continuity. Therefore the perturbation can be chosen to keep the two classes distinct. The proof does not need its optional monohedral alternative.

**Existence of a finite full-side torus for the perturbed pair.** The A6/B5 theorem gives more than an unspecified periodic tiling. For both-six-distinct metrics it constructs explicit four-face flat tori. For a five-distinct partner, its adjacent, separation-two and opposite-repeat branches give explicit full-side tori or a full-side tiling whose intrinsic neighbor determinism supplies a rank-two translation lattice. The opposite-repeat pure-B branch also supplies a full-side torus. In a lattice branch the lattice quotient is compact and contains finitely many tile orbits by local finiteness; quotienting retains indexed complete sides and their endpoint identifications. Passing to a sufficiently large finite-index sublattice, if desired, prevents identifications within the closure of an individual face. Hence a finite indexed polygonal flat torus really is available in every branch. No rank-one conclusion is being silently promoted here.

**Transfer of this single torus.** A side pairing in that new torus may be absent from T and need not respect E. This is allowed: the final pair is unmarked. Its two perturbed numerical lengths are equal. Since all originally unequal lengths were kept unequal, those two original lengths are equal as well. Thus every side pairing is legal after restoring the original indexed polygons. The abstract cell topology and circular vertex links remain those of the same orientable torus. Each vertex retains precisely the same native corner angles and hence angle 2pi. Positive original edges rule out metric collapse. This gives a compact flat torus with the original faces. Its complete simply connected universal cover is Euclidean; the faces lift to genuine original polygons with disjoint interiors covering the plane. The orientation-preserving deck group acts freely. Every nonidentity element is consequently a translation, and the compact torus quotient supplies two independent translation generators. This is an actual periodic escape.

The transfer uses one finite witness after a fixed perturbation. No convergence of periods, uniformly bounded fundamental domains, or unsupported finite-model principle is needed.

**Rank obstruction.** For the repeated A slots i,k, define d(v)=v_[Ai]-v_[Ak]. A separating vector exists exactly when d does not vanish identically on ker M. Finite-dimensional real linear algebra gives (ker M)-annihilator = row(M); therefore failure is exactly rank([M;d])=rank(M), including d=0 when E already joins the two slots. There is only one repeated pair when A has exactly five distinct side lengths. For algebraic coordinates, lengths and unit vectors lie in a finite real algebraic extension, so this is an exact rank computation. The statement remains an ordinary real-linear-algebra theorem for more general real data.

## Attacks, defects, and remaining scope

I specifically tried to invalidate the argument by allowing incompleteness at infinity after remetricization, unrelated seam matchings in the periodic witness, arbitrarily large periods, reflected faces, symmetry-related indexing, accidental congruence of the perturbed prototypes, and a non-full-side periodic dependency. The finite affine templates close the completeness issue; preservation of every original length inequality closes the unrelated-seam issue; the single finite torus closes the period-limit issue. No unresolved defect remains in the stated lemma. I made no changes to the author's proof.

The exact remaining condition for an application is substantial: one must exhibit a defect-free source tiling whose seam-generated relation and closure map admit the stated separating variation. There is no proof here that all A5/B5 pairs admit such a variation. An equivalence class can already join the repeated slots, or closure can annihilate their difference even when they are in different classes. Both rank obstructions are therefore necessary conditions for any hypothetical aperiodic A5/B5 pair, not an exclusion of that entire domain.

No auxiliary coloring theorem is imported or established by this review. The main unrestricted two-polygon problem remains unresolved.
