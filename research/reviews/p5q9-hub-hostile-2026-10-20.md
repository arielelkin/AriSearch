# Hostile review of the P5/Q9 hub gate

Date: 2026-10-20. Claim reviewed: `research/searches/p5q9-hub-gate-2026-10-20.md` and its Python script. **Verdict: the proposed pair is plane-nontilable.** The closure and local angle/length deductions survive, but the reported 64 nonoverlapping cap-plus-owner patches do not. Independent exact certificate: [p5q9-hub-hostile-check-2026-10-20.py](p5q9-hub-hostile-check-2026-10-20.py).

## Checked directly

The closure equation `1+b(u144+u276)+c(u72+u216)=0` follows by solving a two-by-two real system. `b,c` are positive, and the three Q edge blocks are rotations by 120 degrees, so Q closes. The listed exterior turns are strictly between 0 and 180 degrees, giving strict convexity. Every corner is greater than 90 degrees, so a tiling cannot have a side-interior vertex (it would need at least two corners summing to 180 degrees); every tile vertex is trivalent. Direct enumeration of the five angle values gives the four triples in the report. P96 has no all-P triple, and Q156 no all-Q triple, so neither species can tile alone.

For a unit seam, the two possible P/P endpoint pairings are 96+96 and 108+108, or two 96+108 pairs. The first has a 168-degree deficit at one endpoint, absent from the angle palette. The second requires Q156 at both endpoints, but its side lengths {1,c} do not match the exposed {b,c}. Q/Q endpoint deficits are 48, 60, or 72 degrees. At a P/Q seam the swapped choice leaves a 96-degree deficit at Q156 and exposes {c,c}, whereas P96 has incident lengths {1,b}. Thus the P96/Q156 orientation is forced in a full-edge tiling, including reflected tiles.

At each exposed correct hub endpoint, the residual angle is 108 degrees and the adjacent full-edge lengths are {b,c}. The only P corners with that angle and those lengths are P vertices 2 and 3; Q has no 108-degree corner. Both reflection orders are needed. This yields two cap images at each of six endpoints. For the smallest translational `3P+Q` carrier, one Q's three unit sides consume three distinct P translation orbits because P lacks 120-degree symmetry. The twelve cap images have none of those translation orientations, so that specific carrier is excluded. It does not exclude a larger cell.

## Collision-check failure

The script's `overlap` function is not an exact separating-axis test. It uses `u(90)` as a normal, but `u(d)=zeta30**(d//12)`, so `u(90)` is a direction of 84 degrees. It also calls Python `max`/`min` on elements of the unordered cyclotomic field before applying `sgn`; that field's comparison does not agree with its real embedding. For example, in Sage `K(-3)>0` evaluates true while `AA(K(-3))>0` evaluates false. The area print likewise uses `u(270)=u(264)` and is not the polygon area.

I reran the author's finite placement enumeration with only the collision primitive replaced. For an edge `e` and point `x`, the exact projection was `(conj(e)*x-e*conj(x))/(z-conj(z))`, converted to `AA` *before* min/max. This is a positive multiple of the oriented cross product, and edge normals of both convex polygons are tested. The six P caps alone still give 64 ten-tile nonoverlapping patches. However, owner Q for cap positions 0, 2, and 4 under choice 0 overlaps the original four-tile hub. Every one of the 64 sixteen-tile choices has a positive-area collision. The Q-owner placement is the unique opposite-interior placement along P side 0, modulo Q's threefold symmetry. The discrepancy is in collision detection, not in the owner's orientation.

The smaller independent checker reconstructs P and Q, the three arms, and all full-edge P108 caps at Q vertices 1 and 3. It enumerates P vertices 2 and 3, both incident-ray orders and both reflection flags, and obtains exactly two distinct cap images at each endpoint. The owner on each cap's unit side is placed across that side with the opposite reflection parity; using Q side 3 or 6 gives the same image because Q has threefold rotational symmetry. At Q vertex 3, the vertex-2 cap's compulsory Q owner overlaps the four-tile hub. The vertex-3 cap's cap/owner pair overlaps both cap/owner choices at Q vertex 1. Thus **no simultaneous completion of just these two endpoints and their compulsory owners exists**. Positive-area collision is certified by exact separating-axis tests against all edges of both polygons; no duplicate-image identification is used.

## Global consequence for this fixed pair

Suppose a tiling exists. The all-obtuse argument forces it to be edge-to-edge. If it contains a Q, each of that Q's three unit sides must meet a P unit side by the exhaustive seam audit above. At each such seam P96 must meet Q156. Q's three unit sides and its threefold symmetry therefore force exactly the three reflected P arm images used in the independent checker, with no free orientation choice. The checker proves this hub cannot have the two compulsory caps and their unit-edge Q owners, a contradiction. If the tiling contains no Q, it is P-only, which is impossible at every P96 corner by the trivalent angle enumeration. Hence **neither finite-patch extension to the plane nor a plane tiling exists for this exact pair**. This is a restricted candidate-kill result, not an impossibility theorem for arbitrary two-convex-polygon pairs.
