## Exact claim reviewed

Reviewed [the source](../lemmas/hexagon-rank-one-defect-escape-sol-2026-10-19.md), its named defect-limit and single-period escape dependencies, their review records, and the governing research problem and protocol. For two fixed genuine convex hexagons, an unrestricted tiling with specified nonzero translation T implies an edge-to-edge everywhere-trivalent tiling with that same T, and hence a doubly periodic edge-to-edge trivalent tiling. Either species may disappear; reflections are allowed. This is an existence transfer between admitted tilings, not a claim that the original tiling is edge-to-edge or doubly periodic.

## Assumptions and attacks checked

1. **Local finiteness despite arbitrary orientations and tiny partial contacts.** Every tile meeting a bounded set lies in its D-neighborhood; the positive minimum area and disjoint interiors bound the number. A tile has uniformly finitely many touching tiles by the same argument. Polygon pairs have finitely and uniformly boundedly many contact endpoints. Thus planarization and defect counting are legitimate. No positive minimum separation of unrestricted defect points is assumed.

2. **Defect count versus combinatorial incidence count.** In the selected patch for Q_(R+D+1), every tile touching Q_R is selected. In particular the full star of every counted point is interior. A native corner in another side's relative interior splits that native side into an additional incidence piece; distinct geometric points give distinct splits. A degree-at-least-four point contributes at least one positive interior excess-valence term. Counting a point of both types twice only enlarges the bound. Hence the geometric union cardinality is bounded by delta+D_v. The imported exact identity delta+2D_v=6V_b-2q-B-6+6H, with nonnegative delta and D_v and O(R) boundary terms, genuinely implies the required O(R) count. Padding avoids charging incomplete boundary stars. Constants are uniform under translation and depend only on the prototypes.

3. **An unrecorded partial-contact defect.** For two convex polygons with disjoint interiors a positive-length contact is a segment on native sides. Unless it is the whole side of each, at least one endpoint is a native vertex in the relative interior of the other's side. A point contact in a side interior likewise has a native corner there: two side interiors cannot touch at an isolated crossing without an interior overlap. Thus a clean region really has native edge-to-edge contacts. Strictly convex native corners rule out degree two; excluding degrees at least four leaves exactly three corner sectors. A persistent sliding fault therefore supplies defects and cannot evade B.

4. **Unbounded transverse representatives and arbitrary orientation of T.** Each orbit has exactly one representative with longitudinal coordinate in [0,ell). For any finite collection its transverse coordinates have some finite bound H. For R at least H, ell and 1, the displayed longitudinal translates have norm less than 3R and hence lie in the original-coordinate Q_(3R), regardless of T's slope. They are distinct within and between orbits. Dividing the count by R and then letting R grow gives m <= 3C ell/2. The argument fixes the collection before taking the limit; it does not require a bound on all representatives in advance. Since arbitrarily large finite collections are impossible, the orbit set is finite, and its finitely many transverse coordinates give a finite strip. Nonprimitive periods cause no change.

5. **Moving the strip in the wrong direction or losing the fixed period.** Translation by -a_j n sends the strip to transverse coordinate near -a_j, leaving every fixed window and its tile margin eventually clean. All approximants retain precisely the same vector T. The compactness argument uses bounded placements, a finite type set, compact O(2), and uniform local counts. Coverage follows by converging a covering tile for any fixed point; overlap would persist in nearby placements. For each approximated limit tile P, its approximants translated by T converge to P+T and belong to the same configurations. Closure of the placement-family limit gives P+T as a limit tile; -T gives equality. Discrete indexing choices and polygon symmetries cannot destroy this setwise translation symmetry.

6. **New T-junctions or fourfold stars in the clean limit.** The chosen r is positive for fixed genuine convex polygons. Within B(v,r), incident tiles equal their corner sectors. Complete native side matching excludes another native corner along their boundary rays, while disjoint interiors exclude one inside a sector. This supplies uniform native vertex separation throughout every fixed eventually clean window. For any limit native vertex, its approximating three-tile stars have a subsequence with fixed corner slots and types. Full-side endpoint equalities and the sector cover pass to the limit. Their positive-angle interiors exclude an additional tile sector. A new native-corner-to-side-interior contact would require this complete star to lose a side match, but its matching neighbour and endpoints also have convergent bounded placements. Thus side-interior contacts and excess valence cannot arise. The uniform separation addresses vertices of different tiles, rather than merely distinct vertices within one polygon.

7. **Misapplication of the reviewed escape theorem.** The produced limit meets its finite fixed convex prototype, native edge-to-edge, and nonzero T hypotheses. The theorem uses no assertion that the unrestricted source had finitely many orientations. Its cut-and-repeat conclusion is locally identical to source neighborhoods, so trivalence survives. Independently, a sufficiently coarse rank-two period sublattice makes a finite embedded hexagon torus quotient; 2E=6N and Euler give average valence three, and every valence is at least three, forcing trivalence.
