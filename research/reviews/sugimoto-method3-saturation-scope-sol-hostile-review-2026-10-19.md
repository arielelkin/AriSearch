# Method 3 saturation scope: independent hostile review

2026-10-19. Verdict: PASS for the stated parent-contained all-size exclusion, local external-star consequences, and generic counterexample. Reviewed `research/lemmas/sugimoto-method3-saturation-scope-2026-10-19.md` independently of its author. No discovery log was edited.

The saturation lemma is valid. A supporting half-plane at a boundary point of a convex union cannot contain disjoint incident sectors whose total angular measure exceeds 180 degrees, whether or not their union is contiguous. Such a point is interior, so every incident tile having interior arbitrarily close to it must be selected. This applies equally at T contacts. The minimum genuine child angle is 75 degrees; a side-interior sector is 180 degrees.

The all-size parent-contained proof exhausts the segment-contact graph. A convex finite union of more than one full-dimensional tile must have a segment-contact adjacency; mere point joins cannot provide a convex neighborhood connecting interiors. Selecting A/H, B/C or C/D forces an interior point at a parent boundary vertex, impossible for a subset of that parent. Each other graph edge involves H and one of B/C/D; saturation at R or S forces one of the prohibited pairs. Specifically B/H forces C at R, D/H forces C at S, and C/H forces both B and D. Thus no cluster size escapes the argument. Reflections preserve every sector sum.

The claimed unique external children are justified, including under partial contacts. At X8 and X10 the remaining source-complement sector is 120 degrees; at X1 and X5 it is 90 degrees. At least one external child fills each residual sector in a source-derived plane tiling. Two child vertex sectors would require at least 150 degrees; a side-interior sector would require 180. Thus exactly one external child fills each residual sector, with the stated angle. The paper does not identify its surrounding parent or force that whole parent, and the artifact correctly declines that inference.

The regular-hexagon counterexample is valid. Independent exact SymPy checks on the supplied vertices verified the pentagon angle word (120,90,120,120,90), strictly positive turns, and threefold area equality with the parent hexagon. Rotations by 120 degrees give the three pentagons and the three radial seams; their central pair sectors are 240 degrees. The translated honeycomb subdivision also satisfies the proposed interface property: opposite parent sides have opposite parity, so one side of a shared hexagon side may be split at its midpoint while the other remains whole. Every resulting positive-length contact is a half side reaching an original hexagon corner. Each incident child has a genuine 120 degree corner there, yielding 240 degrees for either pair. Thus T contacts do not invalidate the example. Closure can terminate at a finite convex hexagon despite every interface having an over-180 endpoint.

These findings certify no global Method 3 no-go. Arbitrary finite clusters crossing parents remain unresolved, and the exact local external-star result supplies no monotonicity or infinite-path invariant. The source-specific first-gate review remains separate and its earlier scope statement should be read with this additional parent-contained result now reviewed.
