# Two-face weak-hex periodic escape gate

Verdict: all cases in the stated finite model are ruled out after exact ordered-field replay. This is a finite escape attempt, not an impossibility theorem for the pentagon family.

Each native `(60,60,150,120,150)` pentagon has exactly one side split at a strictly interior point, retaining the inserted flat angle as a sixth corner. The enumeration checks five split choices per species, all three index-two Hermite quotients of the standard honeycomb, and all twelve rotated/reflected placements of each weak hexagon. It checks 10,800 cases, of which 288 have all vertex sums equal to 360 degrees. Reflection side indices are determined by their endpoints (`r-k-1`), while corner indices are `r-k`.

The full family produces 24 distinct exact side-equation spaces; the two extra controlled-module equations reduce this to 11. Native parameters, both shortened pieces of each split side, and both `b` sides must be strictly positive. Both known Type 2 monohedral escape loci are excluded for each species. Normalizing `e_A=1` loses no solution because all constraints are homogeneous and `e_A>0`.

The original Z3 nonlinear-real runs returned five UNKNOWN systems for the family and four for the module. Those historical certificates remain unchanged. The separate `*-exact.json` records resolve the same finite domain with Sage's exact real-algebraic ordered-field polyhedra: 24/24 family systems and 11/11 module systems are infeasible, with zero UNKNOWN. The fixed sample had already returned 24/24 UNSAT.

For the exact replay, a positive form in the side-equation row space is forced to zero, immediately contradicting strict positivity. A forbidden Type 2 form in that space likewise rules out the requested nonmonohedral pair. Otherwise the four forbidden forms give sixteen possible strict sign cones. In each cone, every strict form is bounded below by a common variable epsilon, with epsilon at most one. Strict feasibility is equivalent to feasibility with epsilon positive. The polyhedron has no lineality: any lineality vector has zero epsilon and all six shape variables and both cuts zero, because each of these coordinates has a lower bound among the inequalities. The epsilon objective is bounded above. Therefore any feasible point with positive epsilon implies an optimum face containing a vertex with positive epsilon; testing the exact vertices is sufficient. This argument does not substitute a numerical tolerance for a strict inequality.

The retained producer is `research/searches/pentagon-doubleacute-twoface-periodic-attack.py`; the exact certificates are `research/certificates/pentagon-doubleacute-twoface-family-exact.json` and `pentagon-doubleacute-twoface-module-exact.json`. The field runs inspect the same cases as the earlier nonlinear-real runs and do not enlarge their domain.

The model covers two-face trivalent honeycomb quotients with one split side on each pentagon. It omits larger quotients, different contact graphs, multiple cuts, and four-valent flat-plus-three-acute contacts. Consequently its negative result leaves those mechanisms open. A future positive weak torus needs a geometric development check: convex faces with matching lengths and vertex sums of 2 pi form a compact orientable flat surface; its complete simply connected development is the Euclidean plane, and its torus deck group consists of translations. Suppressing the inserted flat corners then gives the original pentagons. The strict-hexagon theorem is not used for flat corners.
